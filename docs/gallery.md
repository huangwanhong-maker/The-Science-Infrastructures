# Gallery

*Status: draft, 2026-10-31.*

These are screenshots of the infrastructure's applications, the three atlases taken on 22 October 2026 from their local servers with the data of that day, the Spiking Simulator and the Complex Atlas on 23 October, the History Complex and the Genome Workbench on 24 October, the Economy Atlas on 24 October and again on 4 November, Cosmopoiesis on 30 October, the Urban Science Workbench on 31 October, and the Body Atlas on 4 November. The search case studies' figures (§13) were drawn on 5 November from their records' results.

- All are web applications without a build step.
- Their science is the provisional JavaScript of the domains they draw on, tested against published references and closed-form answers in 422 checks, and the C++ of the foundations and of the metrology, geodesy, astronomy, geophysics, meteorology, navigation, neuroscience, history, genomics, evolution, economics, robotics, planetary-science, urban-science and anatomy domains, checked the same way.
- Each caption says what is shown, where the data come from, and how the picture is made.

Click an image to see it at full size. To run the applications yourself, see the READMEs of the [Earth Atlas](../applications/earth-atlas/README.md), the [Celestial Atlas](../applications/celestial-atlas/README.md), the [Brain Atlas](../applications/brain-atlas/README.md), the [Spiking Simulator](../applications/spiking-simulator/README.md), the [Complex Atlas](../applications/complex-atlas/README.md), the [History Complex](../applications/history-complex/README.md) and the [Genome Workbench](../applications/genome-workbench/README.md), the [Economy Atlas](../applications/economy-atlas/README.md), [Cosmopoiesis](../applications/cosmopoiesis-lab/README.md), the [Urban Science Workbench](../applications/urban-science-workbench/README.md) and the [Body Atlas](../applications/body-atlas/README.md).

## 1. The Earth Atlas

The atmosphere, the ocean, the land and its people on one globe, or on equal-area and equirectangular maps, at one instant. Each kind of data comes from its own dataset and is joined to the map only in the application.

### 1.3 Prediction: an AI Model Run Here, Tropical Cyclones and Disasters

We run the AI weather model Pangu-Weather (Bi et al. 2023) ourselves, on a laptop's CPU, from NOAA's GFS analysis at 0.25°. A week's forecast takes about half an hour, and becomes a product like NOAA's. Tropical cyclones are found in every forecast's sea-level pressure and drawn beside the observed track and the official forecast ([ADR 0022](../development/decisions/0022-ai-forecast-cyclones-and-disasters.md)).

The atlas also classifies the TerraClimate normals itself, by the rules of Beck et al. (2018). Its classes agree with the published map in 89 % of land cells. The classification is invariant under a group of order 2·(6!)⁴: permutations of the months within each half-year, and the exchange of the halves. ### 1.8 Plug-ins: Algorithms Loaded on Request

A plug-in is a script the atlas loads when asked, runs on the data it holds, and shows on the Earth. It derives from one of four classes, `Diagnostic`, `ForecastModel`, `DataAssimilation` or `Trajectories`, says which fields it needs, and returns products and sketches that the atlas draws like anything else ([ADR 0027](../development/decisions/0027-earth-atlas-plugins.md)). The four built in are shown here, each loaded and run from the panel, which is on the right of every picture: the source of each field, the parameters, Run, and the tables.

## 9. Cosmopoiesis

## 12. Fabrica: Vessels Reconstructed from Images

The Body Atlas, renamed Fabrica, reconstructs the body's structures from images, vessels first ([ADR 0070](../development/decisions/0070-structures-reconstructed-from-images.md)). The work is the medical-imaging domain's, on the new image foundation eikon. A vessel is detected by its shape across scales, delineated at its local half-maximum, thinned to its centrelines and radii, and bounded by a closed surface. Several images of one body are registered by mutual information and fused, and arteries are told from veins by what each modality shows. Every result is held to a reference: the truth of a simulated body, or the experts' labels of a real CT.

### 13.15 A District's Economy: the Problem

The ground floors of the blocks between Swanston and Russell Streets, Flinders Lane and Bourke Street, in Melbourne's Hoddle Grid, are set as one problem for three studies. Their models of the people differ: none, a fluid with its circulation, and a field theory with an action. Each of the 61 frontages takes a kind and its weekday and weekend hours, and eleven links may be made. The value is the week's gross operating surplus: the margin on what passers spend at open frontages, with catalysis between neighbouring kinds from CLUE's co-location quotients and competition along a street. The people come from the City's counters, read as tides by the urban-science domain. A study supplies only the passers at each frontage and hour; the rest is shared ([the record](README.md)). This is a study of methods on a real place, not a planning proposal.

### 13.17 A District's Economy as a Flow

The same district as [the shared problem](../research/experiments/2026-11-05-a-district-economy-the-problem/README.md): the ground floors of the blocks between Swanston and Russell Streets, Flinders Lane and Bourke Street, in Melbourne's Hoddle Grid. Its people are modelled as a conserved flow on the streets: many streams of trips, each choosing its way by a logit over every walk, wider streets preferred as a fluid's flux prefers a wider channel. The flow is calibrated hour by hour to the City's counters, each way, and its net flow is split by the economics domain's circulation into a driven part and eddies round the blocks. The flow is linear in its sources and the value is a sum over the week's hours, so a frontage's best response is exact. A sweep of such best responses, 367 evaluations, finds more than study (1)'s black-box searches find in 20,000. This is a study of methods on a real place, not a planning proposal ([the record](../research/experiments/2026-11-05-a-district-economy-as-a-flow/README.md)).

### 13.18 A District's Economy as a Field Theory

The third study of one shared problem, the ground floors of the blocks between Swanston and Russell Streets, Flinders Lane and Bourke Street, in Melbourne's Hoddle Grid. The people are a field on the district's space–time complex: the domain's district, subdivided to 15 m, crossed with the cycle of the week's 168 hours.
- **The action.** Benamou and Brenier's transport with its Fisher information (macroscopic fluctuation theory), continuity by a Lagrange multiplier, the open frontages' attraction as a potential, and sources calibrated to the City's counters.
- **Its exact solution.** The stationary path and the Gaussian measure are solved exactly in Hodge's modes times the week's lines, and conditioned on the 17 counters line by line.
- **The plan.** It is a field coupled to the people, and a heat bath on its exact local conditionals is compared at equal budgets with study (1)'s black-box searches on heuresis.

This is a study of methods on a real place, not a planning proposal ([the record](../research/experiments/2026-11-05-a-district-economy-as-a-field-theory/README.md)).

## 15. Making the Screenshots

The screenshots were taken with headless Chrome at 1600 × 1000 pixels, driven over the DevTools protocol, and saved as JPEG.

The Earth Atlas keeps its view in the address, so each picture can be reproduced from its URL on the local server. For example:

```
http://127.0.0.1:8790/#p=globe&c=-32,36,3.25&s=wind.10m@gfs-forecast:speed&f=wind.10m@gfs-forecast&t=2026-10-22T12:00Z
```

- `p`: the projection.
- `c`: the longitude, latitude and distance of the view.
- `s`, `f`, `l` and `m`: the surface, the flow, the lines, and the model compared.
- `t`: the instant, in UTC.

The Body Atlas's pictures, at 1680 × 1000 pixels scaled to 1500 wide, were taken from its local server in the same way, each from a link that keeps the mode, the part chosen, the systems, the cut and the view, for example `http://127.0.0.1:8990/#m=sections&sec=sagittal:0:r` or `#m=body&p=FMA7205&v=430,330,1150,40,250,40`, and a key pressed by a script where a picture needed it (the coronal cut, the calibre, the isolation).

The Urban Science Workbench's pictures, at 1500 × 950 pixels, were taken from its local server in the same way, the clock's date and hour, the layers and the view set by a script in the page.

The Spiking Simulator's pictures, at 1500 × 950 pixels, were taken in the same way, each from a link that opens an example, runs it and opens a view, for example `http://127.0.0.1:8830/#preset=brunel-si-fast&run=1&from=300&to=360`: `preset` names the example, `run=1` runs it, `tab` opens a view, `from` and `to` (in ms) the span of the activity shown, and `threads` the threads used. The runs are deterministic, so each picture comes back the same.

The Complex Atlas's pictures, at 1500 × 950 pixels, were taken the same way from links that open a complex, a theory or a passage, for example `http://127.0.0.1:8850/#family=lattice-torus&n=12&m=12` (then **Run** in Fields), `#family=surface&genus=2` (the Net view), `#mode=generation&theory=matrix&order=2&stage=graphs`, `#mode=generation&theory=coloured-simplicial&order=3&stage=graphs` and `#mode=generation&theory=gft-boulatov&order=2&stage=graphs` (then a graph's **Its spin foam** and a vertex), and the passages of the Generation mode for the condensate and the quadrangulation. The engine's runs are seeded, so each picture comes back the same.

The History Complex's pictures, at 1500 × 950 pixels, were taken the same way from links that open a canon, a view and a date, for example `http://127.0.0.1:8870/#canon=europe-1785-1828&date=1814-11-15`, `#canon=vienna-1890-1938&mode=spin&time=events`, `#view=loom&canon=italy-1401-1564&focus=tie:julius2~michelangelo`, `#view=moment&canon=vienna-1890-1938&date=1934-12-31` and `#canon=integrated&time=events`, `#view=loom&canon=athens-470-322bc&focus=tie:alcibiades~athens` and `#view=record&canon=science-1543-1727`; a tour's step is `tour=4` with `open=1`, and the power is computed by its button in Readings. The engine's sampling is seeded, so each picture comes back the same.

The Genome Workbench's pictures, at 1500 × 950 pixels, were taken the same way, from links that open a view, a genome and a window. For example, `http://127.0.0.1:8890/#genome=ncbi:NC_000913.3&begin=3905000&end=3945000` (then the chip **predicted genes**), and `#align` (the example **Two reads of one place**, the schedule **WFA**). Also `#index`, `#reads`, `#assembly` and `#trees`; `#toolbox&tool=align-msa` and `#toolbox&tool=tree-build` (then **Example**); `#data` (then Ctrl K); and `#run`. The night theme is the moon at the foot of the rail. The simulations are seeded, so each picture comes back the same.

The Economy Atlas's pictures, at 1500 × 950 pixels, were taken the same way from links that open a view and what it shows, for example `http://127.0.0.1:8910/#map&year=2024&economy=USA`, `#map&year=2010&arcs=driven`, `#spacetime&from=2015-01&to=2026-06&currency=MXN`, `#production&table=three` and `#circuits&network=five`: `year`, `arcs` (`all`, `driven`, `circulating` or `none`), `shown`, `faces`, `labels`, `flowing`, `economy` (a place's id, ISO alpha-3 for the library's), `colour` (`wb:life_expectancy`, an indicator of the World Bank) and `dataset` for the map; `tool` for the toolbox (`#toolbox&tool=trade-gravity`, `#toolbox&tool=convergence`), each tool running its example, the picture taken once its answer is drawn and scrolled into view; `from`, `to`, `currency` and `dataset` for spacetime; `table` and `network` for the examples. Cosmopoiesis's pictures, at 1500 × 950 pixels, were taken the same way, from a fresh profile, by driving the console from scripts of steps: its focus keys, its decks and pages, the theme, the layers, the figures' keys and their windows, and addresses typed into its address unit (`earth(51.4779 N, 0.0015 W, 92 m)`, `moon(43.31 S, 11.36 W)`, the step from Fuji above, `moon(7.5 N, 59.0 W)` for Reiner Gamma, `moon(26.13 N, 3.63 E)` for Hadley Rille, and points beneath the sea and the Andes).
