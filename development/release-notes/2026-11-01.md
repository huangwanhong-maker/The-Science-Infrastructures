# 2026-11-01

## Abnormal genomes: the international notations, their detection, and expression read from sequence

The Genome Workbench now describes, detects and explains genomes that depart from their reference ([ADR 0064](../decisions/0064-abnormal-genomes-their-description-and-expression.md)).

- **The international notations, read as languages.** Describe reads HGVS (sequence variants) and ISCN (karyotypes, arrays, sequencing results) by their grammars. It draws each description's derivation over its text. A text that is not right is shown exactly how far it is right and what could follow. Its meaning is computed:
  - **A variant.** On a real reference (HBB, CFTR, BRCA1, the mitochondrion), a variant is placed in its three normal forms over the stretch it could occupy (VCF's left form, HGVS's 3′ form, SPDI's whole interval) and carried through every transcript to its protein, for example CFTR's c.1521_1523del to p.(Phe508del). The domain agrees with Mutalyzer, NCBI and VEP on 53 real variants.
  - **A karyotype.** Every chromosome is rebuilt from pieces of the normal ones and painted by the chromosome each piece came from. What is lost, gained and joined is listed from band to band, in GRCh38's bases.
- **Detection.** Rearrangements reads a simulated individual on GRCh38's chromosomes 21 and 22, or on *E. coli*. It finds deletions, duplications, inversions, insertions, translocations, copy number and copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity from paired reads, depth and allele balance. Each call shows its evidence and is written in VCF, HGVS and ISCN. Tumour does the same for a cancer genome against its normal:
  - its somatic mutations;
  - its purity, ploidy and allele-specific copy number by ASCAT;
  - its clones and their tree;
  - the processes that made its mutations, as signatures;
  - microsatellite instability.
- **Expression predicted.**
  - Regulation paints every gene's codon adaptation round a real genome and sets it against measured protein abundance (ρ 0.58 for *E. coli*). It learns σ70's promoter from measured start sites, and learns expression from measured promoters by short words and by a small network, whose filters are read back as motifs.
  - Association analyses a simulated cohort as a study would: the scan, fine-mapping, eQTLs, expression predicted from genotype (PrediXcan), heritability and polygenic scores, each beside its truth.
- **Beneath.** Six new genomics modules and evolution's `association` do this work. The foundations gained what is general:
  - morphe: grammars written in BNF;
  - tyche: change points, penalised regression, factorisations, fine-mapping, variance components, multiple testing and small networks;
  - calx: assignment and multinomial draws.

## New York in the Urban Building Workbench

The Urban Building Workbench now opens New York beside Melbourne ([ADR 0065](../decisions/0065-new-york-in-the-workbench.md)). Lower Manhattan, from the Battery to Canal Street and from the Hudson to the Brooklyn Bridge, stands in three dimensions:
- its buildings at the roof heights New York's own records give, with their light courts;
- its street trees, each sized by its trunk, and its shore with the piers;
- its streets and parks.

The buildings, trees and shore come from Valdrada, Mehmet Kerem Turkcan's open digital twin of the city, fetched at a pinned revision; the streets and parks from OpenStreetMap. The map credits both.

- **Street Oceanography** reads the one open record in Manhattan of walkers counted each way every hour: the city's counter on the Brooklyn Bridge's promenade, 2017 to 2019. Its tide, its two currents (toward Manhattan and toward Brooklyn), its rhythm and its response to New York's weather and holidays are drawn and analysed as Melbourne's streets are. The promenade turns out to have one crest a day, in mid-afternoon, a leisure walk's tide, not a commuter street's two.
- **Landscape Dynamics** works on the Financial District's old streets about Wall Street and Stone Street: 145 junctions, 173 streets and 27 blocks, and 168 buildings sharing 201 walls, to set, rewrite and run like Melbourne's.

Behind this, the data library's city model holds one file for each city. Analyses that serve one city are offered there alone, and switching city starts its district afresh.

## Landscape Dynamics, quieter: one thing at a time

The Urban Building Workbench's Landscape Dynamics has been redrawn to be calmer and easier to read. Each of its four analyses now shows one thing at a time, in tabs: the district in *Complex*, *Freedom* and *Dynamics*; the events, exactly, in *Parts*, *Law*, *Descriptions*, *Interventions* and *Evidence*; scales and memory in *Modes*, *Holes*, *Memory* and *Particles*, each tab drawing its own view on the map; and a building's rooms in *Rooms*, *Heat* and *Changes*. The figures come first, as a short row of numbers; each finding gives its statement and keeps how it was obtained, and its limits, folded beneath it; rates, the day's air and other conditions sit in folds until they are wanted; on and off are switches, and choices are segmented keys. The legends on the map are condensed to a line or two and a row of swatches. A building's changes (doors, a stair, two rooms merged and parted) are now a short list, each with its hour and what it did to the rooms and their heat; the day's air is shared between the building and the district's run.
