# Release Notes: 5 November 2026

## 1. heuresis: a Foundation for Search, Evolutionary Computation First

In this update the infrastructure gains the stage of discovery it lacked: the search for solutions ([ADR 0073](../decisions/0073-the-search-foundation-heuresis.md)). heuresis, a seventh foundation on calx, morphe, physis and tyche, searches spaces too large to enumerate. A domain states its problem (a space, objectives, constraints) and composes heuresis's algorithms; it writes no search of its own.

- **Search that stays pure.** An algorithm asks for evaluations and is told them, and never calls its objective. A domain's simulation can therefore evaluate it on threads, on remote workers, or on a device, and a run is the same wherever and in whatever order its evaluations are made.
- **Open to what comes next.** Each new kind of thing is added without editing what exists:
  - new algorithms and representations;
  - executors for clusters and devices;
  - quantum devices and annealers, as samplers whose laws the CPU emulates exactly;
  - islands spread over machines;
  - proposals from a language model or an agent through the API.
- **Eighteen modules, 492 checks.**
  - The search process, evolutionary algorithms, local search, CMA-ES and differential evolution, swarms, search distributions, quantum search, annealing.
  - Many objectives with exact hypervolumes, quality-diversity, programs typed by physical dimensions, islands, Bayesian optimisation, landscapes and the analysis of runs.
  - Each is held to closed forms, brute force, or pycma.
- **The map of the mathematics** (`docs/mathematics.md` §7b, §9) now says where optimisation lives:
  - calx's when local;
  - morphe's when exact;
  - heuresis's when global, stochastic or black-box.

## 2. Perception and Decision in the Economy Atlas

In this update the Economy Atlas reads the state of each economy from its growth and draws the decisions taken on what it reads ([ADR 0074](../decisions/0074-perception-and-decision-in-the-economy-atlas.md)).

- **Perception.** A new view, Decisions, reads the regime of 212 economies, contraction, normal growth or a boom, from the World Bank's growth of real GDP since 1961. One hidden Markov model is fitted to all of them at once, each economy against its own normal. Contractions last a year and a half on average, normal growth 22 years. The United States was in contraction with chance 0.88 in 2009; in 2020, 41 % of the economies were more likely than not. A year's chances go to the map.
- **Decision.** A policymaker who cannot see the regime, only the year's growth, waits, supports or restrains, as a POMDP solved by HSVI. The page draws the policy over every belief, an economy's beliefs year by year over it, and the interval closing on its value. Beside it are:
  - planning online by Monte Carlo tree search;
  - a fund supporting economies under a year's budget, with the bound on what any allocation can reach, its choice drawn on the map;
  - a budget on support itself, met exactly by a mixture of two plans;
  - two neighbouring policymakers, each seeing only its own growth, who find support worthwhile only together.
- **Agents.** A market that herds without a preference (Kirman), its law against the exact one, and Schelling's sorting tick by tick.
- **Declared, and editable.** What support and restraint do, and what a year is worth, are assumptions. The page states them and lets them be changed.
- **The foundations.** tyche gained hidden Markov models (its decision 0011), nomos Kirman's model (its decision 0005), and the economics domain `regimes` and `policy`. The gallery shows the view in ten pictures (§8.1).
