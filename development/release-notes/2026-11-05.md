# Release Notes: 5 November 2026

## 1. A District's Economy, Planned Three Ways

In this update one planning problem is studied three times, with less and more structure in the model of its people: what the ground floors of a district of Melbourne's Hoddle Grid hold, when they open, and which passages are made, valued by the week's spending they capture.
- **Inputs:** the city's pedestrian counters, read as tides with 46 constituents, and CLUE's establishments.
- **Measured in:** space, time and frequency, with the buildings' relations (party walls, competition, catalysis).

- **Without a model** the people are interpolated from the counters and the evaluation is a black box. The searches find plans worth many times the district as built, but they cannot see that a passage reroutes people, and they create people from nowhere.
- **As a flow**, people are conserved streams on the network: a logit over walks, calibrated to the counters, decomposed into driven flow, eddies round blocks and circulation round holes, with its tides solved line by line. One sweep of best responses that uses the model's linearity, 367 evaluations, beats every black-box run of 20,000.
- **As a field theory**, people are a field with an action S on the district's space–time complex (macroscopic fluctuation theory: transport, Fisher information, continuity by a multiplier). It is solved exactly in the Hodge modes times the week's lines, its fluctuations giving each frontage's uncertainty and a free energy to rank plans. A heat bath on each frontage's exact conditional law beats every black-box run in 4–6 % of their budget.
- **All three** find that the problem's value alone turns the district into a retail monoculture. That is an assumption of the problem (retail beside retail read as catalysis), stated in every record and in the idea that collects what the studies ask of the libraries.
- **heuresis's separable CMA-ES** now samples in O(n), 385 times faster in 621 dimensions.

## 3. Fourteen Case Studies of Search, in the Gallery

In this update the search foundation heuresis is put to work on the domains' problems and on its own algorithms, in fourteen records in `research/experiments/`, each with its question, code, results and caveats, and 59 figures in the gallery's new §13.

- **Discovery.** Programs typed by physical dimensions rediscover Kepler's third law, T = 2π√(a³/GM), from JPL's DE440 on every seed and in both systems of units. Untyped search finds it on at most 2 of 30 seeds.
- **Bodies, places and images.**
  - The robotics domain's rabbit on the Moon is tuned to hop at 0.6 m/s for 28 % less energy per metre than its default gains, with the front of speed against power.
  - The shortest known walk past Melbourne's 134 pedestrian counters is 26.31 km.
  - A district of the Hoddle Grid is redesigned under five objectives from four domains (floor area, winter sun, heating, walking, mix of uses); 192 of NSGA-III's 209 designs beat the district as built in all five.
  - eikon's vessel pipeline, tuned on phantoms, is shown to overfit.
- **The algorithms themselves:**
  - six searches drawn moving over one landscape, with a page that replays them step by step;
  - a COCO-style benchmark of ten continuous searches, and five many-objective searches on three fronts;
  - repertoires of an arm, and NK landscapes enumerated whole from smooth to random;
  - takeover and synchronisation across machines, and Grover adaptive search on the exact law of its device;
  - spiking neurons fitted and their firing classes illuminated;
  - the genetic algorithm shown to be a Wright–Fisher population.
- **What they found to improve** is recorded in each record and in heuresis's next decision.

## 5. heuresis: a Foundation for Search, Evolutionary Computation First

In this update the infrastructure gains the stage of discovery it lacked: the search for solutions ([ADR 0073](../decisions/0073-the-search-foundation-heuresis.md)). heuresis, a seventh foundation on calx, morphe, physis and tyche, searches spaces too large to enumerate. A domain states its problem (a space, objectives, constraints) and composes heuresis's algorithms; it writes no search of its own.

- **Search that stays pure.** An algorithm asks for evaluations and is told them, and never calls its objective. A domain's simulation can therefore evaluate it on threads, on remote workers, or on a device, and a run is the same wherever and in whatever order its evaluations are made.
- **Open to what comes next.** Each new kind of thing is added without editing what exists:
  - new algorithms and representations;
  - executors for clusters and devices;
  - quantum devices and annealers, as samplers whose laws the CPU emulates exactly;
  - islands spread over machines;
  - proposals from a language model or an agent through the API.
- **Eighteen modules, 492 checks.**
  - The search process, evolutionary algorithms, local search, CMA-ES and differential evolution, swarms, search distributions, quantum search, annealing.
  - Many objectives with exact hypervolumes, quality-diversity, programs typed by physical dimensions, islands, Bayesian optimisation, landscapes and the analysis of runs.
  - Each is held to closed forms, brute force, or pycma.
- **The map of the mathematics** (`docs/mathematics.md` §7b, §9) now says where optimisation lives:
  - calx's when local;
  - morphe's when exact;
  - heuresis's when global, stochastic or black-box.

## 6. Perception and Decision in the Economy Atlas

In this update the Economy Atlas reads the state of each economy from its growth and draws the decisions taken on what it reads ([ADR 0074](../decisions/0074-perception-and-decision-in-the-economy-atlas.md)).

- **Perception.** A new view, Decisions, reads the regime of 212 economies, contraction, normal growth or a boom, from the World Bank's growth of real GDP since 1961. One hidden Markov model is fitted to all of them at once, each economy against its own normal. Contractions last a year and a half on average, normal growth 22 years. The United States was in contraction with chance 0.88 in 2009; in 2020, 41 % of the economies were more likely than not. A year's chances go to the map.
- **Decision.** A policymaker who cannot see the regime, only the year's growth, waits, supports or restrains, as a POMDP solved by HSVI. The page draws the policy over every belief, an economy's beliefs year by year over it, and the interval closing on its value. Beside it are:
  - planning online by Monte Carlo tree search;
  - a fund supporting economies under a year's budget, with the bound on what any allocation can reach, its choice drawn on the map;
  - a budget on support itself, met exactly by a mixture of two plans;
  - two neighbouring policymakers, each seeing only its own growth, who find support worthwhile only together.
- **Agents.** A market that herds without a preference (Kirman), its law against the exact one, and Schelling's sorting tick by tick.
- **Declared, and editable.** What support and restraint do, and what a year is worth, are assumptions. The page states them and lets them be changed.
- **The foundations.** tyche gained hidden Markov models (its decision 0011), nomos Kirman's model (its decision 0005), and the economics domain `regimes` and `policy`. The gallery shows the view in ten pictures (§8.1).
