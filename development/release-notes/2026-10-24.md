# Release Notes: 24 October 2026

## 1. Value in Space and Time: the Economics Domain and the Economy Atlas

In this update the infrastructure has an economics domain and an application that draws it ([ADR 0044](../decisions/0044-value-in-space-and-time.md)).

- **Loop deficits.** Value carried between positions is read through its invariants. A loop's deficit is the share of value lost going round, and the deficits all vanish exactly when a system of prices exists. These are the invariants of the user's paper on loop deficits, with the theorem behind them proved.
- **The map.** The Economy Atlas splits the world's trade in goods, year by year from 2010 to 2024, into what economies exchange both ways and what flows net. It splits the net flows, each link carrying in proportion to its gross trade, into what runs from senders to drawers of value, the eddies among neighbouring economies, and the circulation through distant partners. The balances drive about two fifths of the net flows; the rest circulates.
- **Spacetime.** Twenty currencies are followed month by month as a spacetime, each month's loop curved by the departure from interest parity. The atlas shows that interest differentials remove only about 2% of the curvature exchange rates make month by month, while the carry of the higher-yielding currencies persists on average.
- **Production and circuits.** The atlas solves input–output tables as circular flows, and reads the circuits of the paper.
- **Foundations.** The general mathematics went to the foundations: discrete exterior calculus and triangulations of the sphere in physis, conjugate gradients in calx, and the complex of a record of encounters in morphe, on which the history domain now also stands.
- **Data.** The OECD's trade statistics, the ECB's exchange rates and deposit facility rate, and the BIS's policy rates join the data library.

The whole run counts 4689 checks in twenty-eight suites, and every domain passes alone.

## 2. Genomes: the Genomics and Evolution Domains and the Genome Atlas

In this update the infrastructure analyses genomes, in two new domains written in C++ from the start ([ADR 0043](../decisions/0043-the-genome-infrastructure.md)). Genomics reads genomes in their formats and genetic codes; aligns sequences, with the alignment defined once as a sum over an edit graph in a semiring and computed by classical and fast schedules (Gotoh, Myers–Miller, bands, X-drop, Myers' bit vectors, Farrar's stripes and one target to a vector lane, the wavefront algorithm and BiWFA, partial orders), each held to that definition; indexes genomes by suffix arrays and the FMD-index, and compares them by MinHash sketches; counts k-mers and assembles genomes on the de Bruijn graph; maps reads like minimap2 and calls the variants of a diploid; finds genes, CpG islands and repeats; aligns many sequences progressively; and quantifies and tests RNA-seq. Evolution builds trees by distances, likelihood and parsimony with the bootstrap, simulates and reads populations, and measures selection by dN/dS and the McDonald–Kreitman test. Every module is checked against published examples and brute force, and simulation gives each result its truth.

The Genome Atlas shows them, at http://127.0.0.1:8890/ on your computer: a genome's map, with its origin of replication and its genes found from the sequence alone; two sequences aligned with the footprint of each algorithm on the edit graph; a diploid read, mapped and called, with its pileup; an assembly cleaned step by step; an RNA-seq sample quantified and two conditions compared; gene trees, selection and whole-genome sketches; populations; and its engine's own work, kept alive as workers here and on other machines, sharing large jobs out in shards, beside the browser's GPU. Fourteen RefSeq genomes come through the data library; their every protein is translated as RefSeq translates it.

## 3. The History as a Field Theory

In this update the spin foam of a history is computed exactly, and a history can be read as a field theory ([ADR 0042](../decisions/0042-the-history-as-a-field-theory.md)).

- **Exact numbers.** The spins, Wilson loops and reconstructed signs of every single canon are now computed exactly rather than sampled, by physis's new contraction of ℤ₂ gauge theories. Its cost is set by the circles of open ties at a history's densest moment. The Moment view lists the spin networks along which the theory's foams cross a date, with their weights.
- **Fitted couplings.** The couplings are fitted by the exact likelihood of the attested signs, with an interval for each, shown beside the old estimate. The old estimate counted dependent triangles as independent evidence, so balance falls markedly: from 0.95 to 0.60 in the alliances before 1914.
- **The Field view.** The History Complex reads a history as one Feynman graph of a group field theory. Its occasions are interaction vertices, typed and drawn, and its amplitude is exactly 2^(F + E) times the spin foam's weight, checked on every canon. In time, a history is one sample path, whose rates of meeting, of ties begun and ended, and of binding are estimated and tested era by era.
- **Reuse.** The history domain now writes no general method of its own. The contraction, the sampler and the dimensions of SU(2) invariants are physis's; the cycles over ℤ/2, the Q-analysis and the canonical forms of graphs are morphe's. This is the principle the user set for the repository, now in CLAUDE.md.

The whole run counts 2437 checks.

## 4. The History Domain and the History Complex

In this update the infrastructure has its first domain of the humanities, history, and an application that draws it ([ADR 0041](../decisions/0041-the-history-domain.md)). The domain is C++ from the start, and has five parts:

- **The record.** A history is recorded as assertions: occasions, the beings present at them, and the ties between beings, each with its sign, sources, confidence and provenance, in the format of a [codebook](../../domains/history/codebook.md). Dates are kept in the Gregorian, Julian or Old Style calendar their sources use.
- **The complex.** Occasions become vertices, the lives of beings edges, and each tie between two of its meetings a 2-cell, whose homology shows the loops where the record is silent and the closed shells of ties.
- **The spin foam.** The signed ties are read as the spin foam of a ℤ₂ gauge theory fitted to the record, each label derived rather than stipulated ([the theory note](../../domains/history/theory/the-history-complex.md)): spins, amplitudes and the Gauss law, Wilson loops that measure a tie's memory, a reconstruction of the signs the record does not attest, and the power of each being over it.
- **Other complexes.** The shells filled by 3-cells, Dowker's complex of who met whom with Atkin's Q-analysis, and persistent homology in time, which morphe gained for it.
- **Joining.** Canons join into one history only where they agree who a being is, by Wikidata item or by name.

Six seed canons, drafted by a language model and not yet checked against their sources, cover Athens (470–322 BC), Florence and Rome, the Scientific Revolution, Europe from Mozart to Schubert, the European alliances before 1914, and Vienna 1890–1938. The data library checks their beings against Wikidata.

The [History Complex](../../applications/history-complex/README.md) shows them in six views. The foam is the history in three dimensions, lives rising through time, cut at any date and opened into its presences. The loom, the network of a moment, the complex, the record and the readings follow. Each canon has a guided tour. A reader's dates follow the historians' convention, Julian before 1582, so that ancient years end where they should. It runs on one's own computer (the Quick Start). The [gallery](../../docs/gallery.md) shows it in twelve pictures, §6, and the whole run counts 2246 checks.
