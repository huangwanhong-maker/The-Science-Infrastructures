# Release Notes: 4 November 2026

## 1. The Economy Atlas as a Scholarly Instrument

The Economy Atlas is redrawn as a research instrument ([ADR 0068](../decisions/0068-the-economy-atlas-as-a-scholarly-instrument.md)), after a brief the user handed in, its good points taken and others left. Its surfaces are porcelain and its ink graphite; gold marks only what is chosen or in focus, and the data keep their own validated colours. Controls are square-cornered and of one height, numbers are set in tabular figures, and section heads in small capitals on hairlines. The side panels fold away so that the map fills the window; the map names its projection and the place under the pointer, and every legend its source and unit. The address follows the view, so a link draws it again. The map can be saved as a figure composed for publication, with title, legend, source, projection and link, as SVG with its type written in or as PNG. The gallery's pictures were taken again in it.

## 2. A Toolbox of Quantitative Economics, on Two New Foundations

The Economy Atlas answers the ordinary questions of quantitative economics. A fifth view, the Toolbox, holds 27 tools: summaries and correlations; linear regression with robust or clustered errors; logit, probit and Poisson with marginal effects; panels with fixed and random effects and Hausman's test; instrumental variables; differences in differences; the convergence of incomes; autocorrelation, unit roots, ARIMA with forecasts, the Hodrick–Prescott filter, VARs and cointegration; inequality, poverty, concentration and index numbers; the gravity of trade by PPML, the concentration of partners and trade intensity; the Solow model, IS–LM and growth accounting; two-player and zero-sum games, stable matching and voting rules. Each opens on an example and runs at once, on the economies by year (their trade beside eleven of the World Bank's indicators), the currencies by month, a table of one's own or one pasted, and what it finds for the economies can be drawn on the map. On the 2024 trade, gravity's distance elasticity is −0.94 by PPML, against −1.46 by least squares on logarithms, which must drop the 11,164 pairs that do not trade.

Two foundations carry it ([ADR 0048](../decisions/0048-the-inference-foundation-tyche.md), [ADR 0056](../decisions/0056-nomos-and-a-toolbox-of-quantitative-economics.md)): tyche, for statistics, and nomos, new, for the methods of the social sciences (measurement, index numbers, games, matching, social choice and econometrics, 242 checks). The economics domain gained growth, the short run and trade (109 checks). The map finds a place, plays through the years, colours the economies by the flows' measures or by any of the World Bank's indicators on a scale no outlier can wash out, shows a place's years, and gives its numbers and its drawing away as CSV and SVG; its colours are lighter. tyche's Hodrick–Prescott filter now solves for the cycle first and is exact to rounding. The gallery's Economy Atlas pictures were taken again, with three of the toolbox and the map's new colouring.

## 3. Cosmopoiesis as a Submodule

Cosmopoiesis is now part of the infrastructure's repository as a git submodule at `applications/cosmopoiesis-lab`, beside the foundations and the neuroscience domain. It keeps its own repository, history and branch; the infrastructure names the commit it stands on. A clone made with `--recurse-submodules` now brings Cosmopoiesis with everything its engine computes on, so the whole of it runs from one clone.
