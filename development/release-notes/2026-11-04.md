# Release Notes: 4 November 2026

## 1. The Names of Places, Areas in Dispute, and a 2D GIS

The maps no longer draw names that are in dispute ([ADR 0069](../decisions/0069-names-of-places-and-a-2d-gis.md)). Every product and map now names places by one policy, a file of the data library.

- **The usage of international statistics.** Taiwan is Chinese Taipei, as the WTO and the OECD name it. Hong Kong and Macao are Hong Kong, China and Macao, China, and Palestine is the West Bank and Gaza. Kosovo is Kosovo (UNSCR 1244). Turkey, the Czech Republic and Swaziland have their current names. The old names still find their places.
- **No name for what is in dispute.** A map labels neither a territory whose status is in dispute nor a sea whose name is: the Sea of Japan, the Persian Gulf, the Gulf of Mexico, the South China Sea and the Korea Strait. Northern Cyprus and Somaliland have no name at all. The gazetteer that Cosmopoiesis draws on its globe lists none of them as a country.
- **Areas in dispute in no one's colour.** Crimea, the Golan Heights, the southern Kurils, Kashmir, Aksai Chin, Arunachal Pradesh, Western Sahara, Abkhazia and the other areas Natural Earth lists are drawn hatched and unnamed over the Economy Atlas's map. Whatever colours the countries does not colour them. A city in one has no country. Jerusalem, Taipei and Pristina are not listed as national capitals; Nicosia is, as Cyprus's.
- **The text agrees.** The economics notes and the gallery's captions say Chinese Taipei, and seas in dispute are described rather than named.
- **A 2D GIS.** The visualization layer now has maps of the sphere in the plane: projections and great-circle arcs, zoom and pan, names placed without overlap, the areas in dispute, and figures for publication. The Economy Atlas's map stands on it, so another application's map need not be written again.

## 2. The Body Atlas, and the Anatomy Domain

The infrastructure has its first atlas of the whole human body, the *Body Atlas*, and a domain beneath it, anatomy ([ADR 0066](../decisions/0066-the-anatomy-domain-and-the-body-atlas.md)). The body is BodyParts3D's adult man: 2,234 parts, each named by the Foundational Model of Anatomy (CC BY 4.0).

- **The whole body, by its systems.** Each of seventeen systems (skin, skeleton, muscles, heart, arteries, veins, brain and nerves, the organs) can be shown, drawn as glass or hidden. Parts are browsed by what they belong to or by their kind, or found by name. A part chosen is lit in rose gold and shows through whatever lies in front of it.
- **A card for every part.**
  - What is known of it: its Latin name, what it does, its blood supply and drainage, its nerves, a clinical note, with references.
  - Where it lies: its vertebral level, its height, its side.
  - What it measures, beside the volume the database published: volume, surface, length × breadth × thickness, form.
  - What it touches, and its place in both of FMA's trees.
  - For a vessel or another long part, its calibre along its length.
- **Cut in the planes of imaging.** A cut by a transverse, coronal or sagittal plane shows its face. The Sections mode draws the cut as an anatomical plate, seen as a CT scan sees it or from above, its parts named on leader lines. Levels are named by the vertebra, read from the body's own vertebrae.
- **Four tours:** the body in layers; the heart and the great vessels; the way back from the foot, the venous return to the right atrium; a walk down the trunk in sections.
- **Silver white,** the user's choice, by day; silver on graphite by night.
- **The anatomy domain**, C++ from the start, holds the body's frames (DICOM's LPS, NIfTI's RAS, the displays of imaging), FMA's parts and the systems of the body, and each part's measures and sections. Its 77 checks include the volumes against the database's (a median ratio of 1.0004) and Cavalieri's principle on the left kidney, to 6 × 10⁻⁶. Its general mathematics went to physis (triangle surfaces, their sections, the solids they bound) and morphe (meshes read as surfaces, and their orientation).
- **What the checks found in the data.** BodyParts3D's part-of tree is a spanning tree of FMA's part-of graph. One pair of muscles has its sides swapped. Near the midline, "left" and "right" in a name may name a chamber or a branch: the right ventricle lies left of the midline.

Next come the veins reconstructed from images, and the flow in them.

## 3. Event Spacetime: a City's Events as a 2-Complex, in the Urban Science Workbench

The Urban Building Workbench is now the Urban Science Workbench, at the user's word, and it has a third plug-in, *Event Spacetime* ([ADR 0067](../decisions/0067-event-spacetime.md)). It draws a district's record of real events as a 2-complex in spacetime: its volumes rise as worldlines (buildings, the spaces of streets and of junctions), the interfaces between them stand as sheets (party walls, frontages, the ends of streets) cut at the events that meet them, and the events are vertices at their place and date.

- **Melbourne's Hoddle Grid, 2002–2024, from real records.** The first district is Landscape Dynamics'. The data library now compiles its record from the City of Melbourne's census of land use (every building and business by property, every year since 2002), the Development Activity Monitor, the Victorian Heritage Register and the event permits of 2014–2018. Every fact is attached by identifier to the building or street it names, and what cannot be attached is counted with its reason.
- **Two views of one complex.** Each event can be seen split into the volumes it touches, joined along the interfaces it meets, or as a single vertex into which its worldlines bend. A plane at the clock's date carries the network of what stood then. One analysis gives what the record holds and left out, and the cells and homology of both complexes. Melbourne's has 9,038 events and censuses as vertices, and its refinement 628 closed shells about 1,219 hinges, where two buildings sharing a wall front one street.
- **The city seen through.** A slider in the rail lowers the city's opacity, for every plug-in, so that what is drawn above it or within it shows through the towers.
- **One network of buildings and streets.** Urban science now stands on architecture, as the bridge between them. Its new module `events` holds the record, builds the complexes on morphe's worldline complexes and joins buildings and streets as one network of volumes and interfaces.

The design, with the formalism for the planned paper *Event-Based 3D GIS on a 2-Complex: A Spin-Foam Approach*, is in [the ideas](../ideas/2026-11-04-event-based-gis-on-a-2-complex.md). The amplitudes and their modes come next.

## 4. The Economy Atlas as a Scholarly Instrument

The Economy Atlas is redrawn as a research instrument ([ADR 0068](../decisions/0068-the-economy-atlas-as-a-scholarly-instrument.md)), after a brief the user handed in, its good points taken and others left. Its surfaces are porcelain and its ink graphite; gold marks only what is chosen or in focus, and the data keep their own validated colours. Controls are square-cornered and of one height, numbers are set in tabular figures, and section heads in small capitals on hairlines. The side panels fold away so that the map fills the window; the map names its projection and the place under the pointer, and every legend its source and unit. The address follows the view, so a link draws it again. The map can be saved as a figure composed for publication, with title, legend, source, projection and link, as SVG with its type written in or as PNG. The gallery's pictures were taken again in it.

## 5. A Toolbox of Quantitative Economics, on Two New Foundations

The Economy Atlas answers the ordinary questions of quantitative economics. A fifth view, the Toolbox, holds 27 tools: summaries and correlations; linear regression with robust or clustered errors; logit, probit and Poisson with marginal effects; panels with fixed and random effects and Hausman's test; instrumental variables; differences in differences; the convergence of incomes; autocorrelation, unit roots, ARIMA with forecasts, the Hodrick–Prescott filter, VARs and cointegration; inequality, poverty, concentration and index numbers; the gravity of trade by PPML, the concentration of partners and trade intensity; the Solow model, IS–LM and growth accounting; two-player and zero-sum games, stable matching and voting rules. Each opens on an example and runs at once, on the economies by year (their trade beside eleven of the World Bank's indicators), the currencies by month, a table of one's own or one pasted, and what it finds for the economies can be drawn on the map. On the 2024 trade, gravity's distance elasticity is −0.94 by PPML, against −1.46 by least squares on logarithms, which must drop the 11,164 pairs that do not trade.

Two foundations carry it ([ADR 0048](../decisions/0048-the-inference-foundation-tyche.md), [ADR 0056](../decisions/0056-nomos-and-a-toolbox-of-quantitative-economics.md)): tyche, for statistics, and nomos, new, for the methods of the social sciences (measurement, index numbers, games, matching, social choice and econometrics, 242 checks). The economics domain gained growth, the short run and trade (109 checks). The map finds a place, plays through the years, colours the economies by the flows' measures or by any of the World Bank's indicators on a scale no outlier can wash out, shows a place's years, and gives its numbers and its drawing away as CSV and SVG; its colours are lighter. tyche's Hodrick–Prescott filter now solves for the cycle first and is exact to rounding. The gallery's Economy Atlas pictures were taken again, with three of the toolbox and the map's new colouring.

## 6. Cosmopoiesis as a Submodule

Cosmopoiesis is now part of the infrastructure's repository as a git submodule at `applications/cosmopoiesis-lab`, beside the foundations and the neuroscience domain. It keeps its own repository, history and branch; the infrastructure names the commit it stands on. A clone made with `--recurse-submodules` now brings Cosmopoiesis with everything its engine computes on, so the whole of it runs from one clone.
