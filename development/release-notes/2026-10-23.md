# Release Notes: 23 October 2026

## 1. Complexes, Symmetry and Fields; the Complex Atlas

In this update the foundations hold the mathematics and physics of complexes and the fields on them ([ADR 0039](../decisions/0039-complexes-symmetry-and-fields.md)): morphe's exact algebra and topology, physis's symmetry, spacetime, field theory, gauge theory and quantum gravity, and calx's forward differentiation. The new [Complex Atlas](../../applications/complex-atlas/README.md) shows them. A complex comes from twenty-five families or is typed as words, facets or JSON, and is drawn in the round, as a net of polygons or as the diagram of its cells. The atlas reads its homology with generators drawn on it, its fundamental group, the surface or manifold it is and whether it collapses; runs gauge fields on it, compared with exact values; and sums the Turaev–Viro invariant of a 3-manifold. It runs on one's own computer (the Quick Start). The map of the mathematics names the new modules, and the whole run counts 1698 checks.

## 2. The Simulator in the Quick Start

In this update the README's Quick Start shows how to run the Spiking Simulator on one's own computer: the compiler it needs and the two lines that build its engine and serve it. It also says why the simulator is not run on our online server, as the atlases are: each run is a simulation computed by the machine that serves it, and the other applications already take much of that server's resources. The engine's build now falls back when a compiler refuses `-march=native`.

## 3. Spiking Networks in the Gallery

In this update the [gallery](../../docs/gallery.md) shows the Spiking Simulator at work, §4. It has Brunel's balanced network in its four states: asynchronous, a fast 187.5 Hz oscillation that each neuron joins one volley in three, firing in lockstep at 332 Hz, and a slow 19.5 Hz rhythm. It also has the classes of excitability as f–I curves from a single run, quantum emitters giving photons that never come in pairs, a neuron slowing as its synapses learn by STDP, an adapting neuron, and a network drawn from its specification. Every number in the captions is the run's own. Links can now open an example on a chosen view, span of time and number of threads, the STDP example learns visibly over a minute, and the profile tells waiting caused by short windows from waiting caused by a shared processor.

## 4. The Simulator's Visualizer and Profiler

In this update the [Spiking Simulator](../../applications/spiking-simulator/README.md) shows what a run did. It draws the spikes as a raster over the population rates, to be looked at more closely by dragging across them. It gives each population's rates, irregularity, synchrony and rhythm, with the spectra and the distributions of intervals and rates behind them, and the f–I curve wherever each neuron had its own current. It traces recorded states and plastic weights, draws the network as a diagram, and profiles the engine phase by phase and thread by thread, with advice when a network is too small for its threads. The profile found the engine's Poisson input to be slow; the fix makes Brunel's network 1.5 times faster. calx gained the fast Fourier transform and Welch's spectra on the way.

## 5. The Spiking Simulator

In this update the project has a fourth application, the [Spiking Simulator](../../applications/spiking-simulator/README.md) ([ADR 0038](../decisions/0038-the-spiking-simulator.md)). In a page, one can compose a network of spiking neurons from the catalogue of twenty-two models, classical and quantum, connect, drive and record it, and run it in the neuroscience domain's C++ engine on one's own computer. Every network is the engine's own specification, with its quantities in units, so it can be saved and run from the command line as well. Eight examples open it: an adapting neuron, Brunel's balanced network in its four states, the classes of excitability in one run, quantum emitters, and a neuron learning by STDP. The engine is built once with g++, from the page or by the recipe.

## 6. A Quantum Neuron

In this update physis begins its quantum theory with the open qubit, and the neuroscience domain's simulator has its first quantum neuron: a driven emitter whose detected photons are its spikes, simulated along exact quantum trajectories that average to the master equation. The plan now also says how networks will later leave the simulator: compiled through calx to GPUs, neuromorphic chips and quantum processors, with the simulator as their reference; that part is not built.

## 7. Seven New Neuron Models

In this update the neuroscience domain's simulator of spiking networks has twenty-one neuron models. Among the new ones are the adaptive LIF and balanced resonate-and-fire neurons of deep spiking networks, the generalised integrate-and-fire neuron fitted to recorded cells, and four classic models of how a neuron begins to fire, each checked against the bifurcation that defines its class.

## 8. The Mathematics, the First C++, and Spiking Networks

In this update the project's mathematics has a map ([docs/mathematics.md](../../docs/mathematics.md), [ADR 0035](../decisions/0035-the-mathematical-foundations.md)). Every area, from logic, category theory and topology to differential equations, linear algebra, statistics and quantum theory, has its place in a foundation, checked against the whole Mathematics Subject Classification.

The project also has its first C++:

- calx's numerical methods;
- physis's units and dynamical systems;
- a simulator of spiking neural networks in the neuroscience domain ([ADR 0036](../decisions/0036-spiking-networks.md)), with fourteen neuron models and learning synapses. It gives the same spikes on any number of threads, runs Brunel's network of 12,500 neurons at 2.6 times real time on a laptop, and agrees with mean-field theory.

The checks now build and run the C++ alongside the JavaScript, and every repository keeps release notes like these.

## 9. Biosignals on the Brain

In this update the Brain Atlas can show recordings of the living brain on the template ([ADR 0034](../decisions/0034-biosignals-on-the-brain.md)). EEG is carried from the scalp to the cortex: the rhythm of a band, the potential at an instant, or the contrast of two conditions, with the traces playing beneath. One current's EEG and MEG patterns can be compared side by side. fMRI maps are read through the cortex, with their peaks named by the anatomy.
