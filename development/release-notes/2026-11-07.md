# Release Notes: 7 November 2026

## 1. Quantitative Economics in the Urban Science Workbench

In this update the toolbox of quantitative economics comes to the Urban Science Workbench, and what it finds of places stands on the map ([ADR 0080](../decisions/0080-quantitative-economics-in-the-urban-workbench.md)). See the [gallery's §18](../../docs/gallery.md).

- **Questions in plain words.** Under a new lens, *Economy*, a place's questions are asked in a click: where Melbourne's walkers came back after the lockdowns, where food and drink cluster, which blocks hold more businesses than their floor space explains, whether poorer economies grow faster. Each says in a sentence what it shows and what it does not.
- **Every tool on the place's own tables.** The tables are Melbourne's blocks by census year, its counters by day with the weather and by month since 2009, Freetown's wards, the cells around the point looked at, the world's economies, what the studio shows, or a table pasted.
- **For each place.** Any tool can be run for each place, each moment, or both, and its numbers come back as a map.
- **Answers on the map.** Residuals, a panel's effects and a tool's numbers for each place become records of the line of time, with their card and a list of the highest and lowest. A city's blocks lie as a carpet beneath the towers, and counters stand as pillars.
- **Neighbours tested.** Moran's I, with hot and cold spots, is a tool of its own. A regression whose rows are places tests its residuals too, and says when its standard errors are too small. Location quotients show where each use gathers.
- **One toolbox for every application.** The Economy Atlas's toolbox now lives in the API, the visualization layer and the data library, so that the atlas and the workbench answer the same tools. The atlas gains Moran's I and location quotients.
- **What works better.** A question asked while the map was loading tiles used to wait behind them for minutes. Tiles now go at low priority and a person's requests at high. The inequality tool also gives what holds when some places hold nothing, where it used to refuse.

## 2. Data in Space and Time, and the Older Works Found Again

In this update anything with a place and a time can go on the Urban Science Workbench's map and run through time, with one's own code over it, and the works built before stand under their names again ([ADR 0079](../decisions/0079-data-in-space-and-time.md)). See the [gallery's §17](../../docs/gallery.md).

- **The older works, named again.** Street Oceanography, Landscape Dynamics, Event Spacetime and Human flow had not gone; the question-first page had only hidden them. The list now has two orders, by question and by work. A place's works stand under its name, what belongs to another place says where it is and goes there, and the search also finds ways of seeing.
- **Your own data.** Drop a table, GeoJSON or a record on the page, and the studio opens with it on the map and on a line of time above the clock. Its columns are told by their names and contents. What cannot be placed is left out and counted on its card. The library also offers earthquakes since 1976, this month's earthquakes, the last three years' tropical cyclones and Melbourne's walkers through a mean week.
- **Your own code.** A script in JavaScript, Python or C++ is given the records shown, its parameters and the view, and answers records, tables, curves and notes under one contract. C++ compiles on first use against the foundations and the domains. There is an example in each language: a density, counts on H3 cells and the speed along tracks. Python and C++ run only for the workbench's own page at this computer's own address.
- **Marks that live in time.** Events open as rings and fade, tracks are fine threads drawn up to the moment, and scrubbing costs the page next to nothing, because the moment is one value on the graphics card. Among a city's buildings a thread stays faintly seen where one stands in front of it. Any plug-in reaches the same line of time by `ctx.st.show(record)`.
- **What works better.** A layer that dims the city now gives it back when it is turned off, where Event Spacetime's foam used to leave the buildings half seen in later sessions. Compiling a C++ plug-in over WSL now takes 23 s, where it did not finish in 291 s. A legend's colour ramp no longer breaks across two lines.

## 3. The Whole Earth in the Urban Science Workbench

In this update the Urban Science Workbench becomes one three-dimensional GIS from the whole Earth down to a doorstep, and any city in the world stands on it ([ADR 0078](../decisions/0078-the-whole-earth-in-the-urban-workbench.md)). See the [gallery's §16](../../docs/gallery.md).

- **Any place.** Fly to a name, or simply come down anywhere: the place opens with its name, and its buildings, roads, water and open land rise on the real terrain. Nothing has to be prepared for it. Kathmandu opens with about 154,000 buildings. A building's height is recorded where one is known, and otherwise an estimate, drawn fainter and saying so when picked.
- **A page that asks a question first.** Five lenses (People, Movement, Fabric, Risk, Change) replace the list of plug-ins, each layer showing the scales at which it means something. An altitude scale, a compass, a day ribbon drawn from the real Sun and folded legends surround the map. It is pink and purple now, on a plum night or lilac paper, and the Earth's imagery is graded to match.
- **The world's development data, on the map.** The plug-in *The territory* draws where people live, poverty and inequality by economy, a region's people, night lights and built land year by year, urbanisation, settlement spreading from 1985 to 2015, river floods by return period, and any World Bank indicator found by what it means. One can also weigh one's own index of a region. Its *Sources* tool says what was taken from each tool and dataset on the list, what was left aside, and why.
- **A city's flood risk.** The plug-in *Flood risk* draws Freetown's assessment: 1 m flood maps, the 153,435 buildings against them, wards' annual losses and the places the Council records as flooding. Recomputed from its 1,943 modelled events, the annual loss is $808,904.91, the assessment's own figure to the cent. ThinkHazard!'s levels can be asked for a unit anywhere.
- **Reach and use.** Pick a place anywhere, and a wave of minutes runs down the streets from it, on foot or by road. In Melbourne, the walkers' share of each part of the centre is set against their habit, following the clock's date.
- **Areas in dispute.** On the mosaics by economy, a hexagon holding any part of an area in dispute takes no economy's value or name.
- **Beneath it.** The urban-science domain gains `exposure`, `access` and `usage`; the data library reads PMTiles and shapefiles and asks world services on demand; the shared GIS layer gains the Earth beneath a city and the world's tiles, for any application.
