Data and method

Sun, Moon and planets
JPL Planetary and Lunar Ephemeris DE440 (Park et al. 2021, AJ 161, 105), read directly from the SPK kernel distributed by NAIF. Valid 1849–2150.
Stars
HYG Stellar Database v4.1 (Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star and Gliese catalogues), CC BY-SA 4.0. Distances from Hipparcos parallaxes.
Constellations
Figures and names from d3-celestial by Olaf Frohn, BSD 3-Clause.
Maps of the bodies and the sky
Solar System Scope textures (INOVE), CC BY 4.0, from NASA, JPL and ESA mission data; the Earth from NASA's Blue Marble and Black Marble (NASA GIBS). Bodies are turned by the IAU rotation models (Archinal et al. 2018), which agree with JPL Horizons to within 0.011°.
Detail at close range
The Moon from the LRO WAC global mosaic (NASA/GSFC/Arizona State University), Mars from the Viking MDIM 2.1 colour mosaic (NASA/USGS), Mercury from the MESSENGER MDIS mosaic (NASA/JHUAPL/Carnegie Institution), as tiles from NASA's Moon, Mars and Mercury Trek.
Corrections
Light-time, precession (IAU 1976), topocentric parallax and refraction are applied. Aberration, nutation and light deflection are not; positions on the sky are good to well under an arcminute.
Time
UTC with leap seconds; TT and TDB for the ephemeris; UT1 taken equal to UTC. Before 1972, ΔT from Espenak and Meeus (2006).
