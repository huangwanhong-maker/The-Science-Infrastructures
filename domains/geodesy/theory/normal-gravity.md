# The Normal Gravity of a Level Ellipsoid

"HM" refers to Heiskanen and Moritz (1967), whose equation numbers Moritz's *Geodetic Reference System 1980* cites; "NGA" to NGA.STND.0036_1.0.0_WGS84 (2014), chapters 3 and 4; "HWM" to Hofmann-Wellenhof and Moritz (2006).

## 1. The Reference Field of the Earth

Gravity on the Earth is the attraction of its masses together with the centrifugal acceleration of its rotation; its potential is $W = V + \Phi$, $\Phi = \tfrac12 \omega^2 (x^2 + y^2)$ in axes fixed to the Earth. The real field is irregular at every scale. Geodesy therefore splits it into a *normal* field $U$, simple and known in closed form, and a small *disturbing* potential $T = W - U$, which carries everything particular to the Earth (the geoid, gravity anomalies, deflections of the vertical). The normal field is the one generated by an ellipsoid of revolution that is itself a level surface of the field, rotating with the Earth and enclosing its mass: a *level ellipsoid*. It matches the figure of the Earth to a few parts in 10⁵ and its gravity to a few parts in 10⁵, so the residual is small and the reference is unambiguous.

**Theorem (Stokes, Poincaré; Moritz 2000, §2).** Given an ellipsoid of revolution with semi-axes $a > b$, the mass $M$ it encloses and its angular velocity $\omega$, there is exactly one function $U$ that is harmonic outside it apart from the centrifugal term ($\nabla^2 (U - \Phi) = 0$), tends to $GM/r$ at infinity, and is constant on its surface; it does not depend on how the mass is distributed inside.

So four constants fix the field. WGS 84 takes $a$, $f = (a-b)/a$, $GM$ and $\omega$ (NGA Table 3.1); GRS 80 takes $a$, $GM$, $\omega$ and the dynamical form factor $J_2$ (IUGG 1979, Resolution 7).

## 2. Ellipsoidal-Harmonic Coordinates

**Definition 1 (NGA Fig. 4.1).** With $E = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ the linear eccentricity, a point has ellipsoidal-harmonic coordinates $(u, \beta, \lambda)$ when

$$ x = \sqrt{u^2 + E^2}\cos\beta\cos\lambda, \qquad y = \sqrt{u^2 + E^2}\cos\beta\sin\lambda, \qquad z = u \sin\beta. $$

The surfaces $u = $ const are the ellipsoids confocal with the reference one ($u = b$), $u$ their semi-minor axis, and $\beta$ the reduced latitude on them.

**Proposition 1 (NGA eqs. 4-8, 4-9).** With $d = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - E^2$,

$$ u^2 = \tfrac12\left(d + \sqrt{d^2 + 4E^2 z^2}\right), \qquad \beta = \arctan\frac{z\sqrt{u^2 + E^2}}{u\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}}. $$

*Proof.* Eliminating $\beta$, $\frac{x^2 + y^2}{u^2 + E^2} + \frac{z^2}{u^2} = 1$, which is $u^4 - d\,u^2 - E^2 z^2 = 0$; its one positive root is the formula. ∎

*Remark.* Where $d < 0$ (more than 5 800 km below the surface) the same root is computed as $u^2 = 2E^2 z^2/(\sqrt{d^2 + 4E^2z^2} - d)$, without cancellation; $u = 0$ is the focal disc, where the coordinates fail.

The coordinates are orthogonal, with scale factors

$$ h_u = w, \qquad h_\beta = w\sqrt{u^2 + E^2}, \qquad h_\lambda = \sqrt{u^2 + E^2}\cos\beta, \qquad w = \sqrt{\frac{u^2 + E^2\sin^2\beta}{u^2 + E^2}} \;\; \text{(NGA eq. 4-10)}. $$

## 3. The Normal Potential

Let $x = E/u$ and

$$ q(x) = \tfrac12\left[\left(1 + \frac{3}{x^2}\right)\arctan x - \frac{3}{x}\right], \qquad q'(x) = 3\left(1 + \frac{1}{x^2}\right)\left(1 - \frac{\arctan x}{x}\right) - 1 \quad \text{(NGA eqs. 4-11, 4-13; HM 2-58, 2-67)}, $$

and $q_0 = q(e')$, $q_0' = q'(e')$ on the reference ellipsoid, where $e' = E/b$ is the second eccentricity.

**Proposition 2 (HWM eq. 2-126).** The normal potential is

$$ U(u, \beta) = \frac{GM}{E}\arctan\frac{E}{u} + \frac12\,\omega^2 a^2\,\frac{q}{q_0}\left(\sin^2\beta - \frac13\right) + \frac12\,\omega^2\,(u^2 + E^2)\cos^2\beta, $$

and its value on the ellipsoid is $U_0 = \frac{GM}{E}\arctan e' + \frac13\omega^2 a^2$ (HM 2-61).

*Proof.* The last term is $\Phi$. Laplace's equation separates in these coordinates; its solutions regular at infinity and symmetric about the axis are $Q_n(iu/E)\,P_n(\sin\beta)$, with $Q_n$ Legendre's functions of the second kind, and $Q_0 \propto \arctan(E/u)$, $Q_2 \propto q(E/u)$ (HM, chapter 1 on ellipsoidal harmonics, and §§2-7 to 2-10). So $V = \frac{GM}{E}\arctan\frac{E}{u} + A\, q(E/u)\,P_2(\sin\beta)$ with $P_2(s) = \frac12(3s^2 - 1)$; the first term tends to $GM/r$. On $u = b$, $\Phi = \frac12\omega^2 a^2\cos^2\beta = \frac13\omega^2 a^2\,(1 - P_2(\sin\beta))$, so $U$ is constant there exactly when $A q_0 = \frac13\omega^2 a^2$; then $A\,q\,P_2 = \frac12\omega^2 a^2 (q/q_0)(\sin^2\beta - \frac13)$, and $U_0 = \frac{GM}{E}\arctan\frac{E}{b} + \frac13\omega^2 a^2$. Uniqueness is the theorem of §1. ∎

**Proposition 3 (the series).** For $0 < x < 1$,

$$ q(x) = \sum_{n \ge 1} (-1)^{n+1}\frac{2n\, x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)(2n+3)}, \qquad q'(x) = \sum_{n \ge 1} (-1)^{n+1}\frac{6\, x^{2n}}{(2n+1)(2n+3)}, $$

alternating series of decreasing terms, so that each is within its first omitted term of its partial sum.

*Proof.* Insert $\arctan x = \sum_{n \ge 0} (-1)^n x^{2n+1}/(2n+1)$: in $q$ the terms of orders $x^{-1}$ and $x$ cancel, and the coefficient of $x^{2n+1}$ is $\frac12(-1)^n\left[\frac{1}{2n+1} - \frac{3}{2n+3}\right] = (-1)^{n+1}\frac{2n}{(2n+1)(2n+3)}$; in $q'$, $1 - \frac{\arctan x}{x} = \sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n+1}\frac{x^{2n}}{2n+1}$, and the coefficient of $x^{2n}$ in $3(1 + x^{-2})$ times it, less 1, is $3(-1)^{n+1}\left[\frac{1}{2n+1} - \frac{1}{2n+3}\right]$. The ratio of successive terms is below $x^2$ for $q'$ and below $\frac67 x^2$ for $q$, which is less than 1; Leibniz's bound follows. ∎

*Rationale.* Outside the ellipsoid $x \le e' = 0.082$, and the closed forms subtract numbers near $3/x \approx 37$ to leave $q \approx 2x^3/15 \approx 7 \times 10^{-5}$: in double precision they keep about eleven figures. The series keep all sixteen; Moritz gives $2q_0$ by the same series (§3). The module sums them below $x = \frac12$ and uses the closed forms above, where they keep about 14 figures. boule (Fatiando a Terra) uses the closed forms, which is why its $\gamma_e$ and $\gamma_p$ differ from these by up to $2.4 \times 10^{-13}$.

## 4. The Derived Constants

With $m = \omega^2 a^2 b/GM$ (HM 2-70), Moritz (2000, §3) gives in closed form

$$ \gamma_e = \frac{GM}{ab}\left(1 - m - \frac{m}{6}\,\frac{e' q_0'}{q_0}\right), \qquad \gamma_p = \frac{GM}{a^2}\left(1 + \frac{m}{3}\,\frac{e' q_0'}{q_0}\right) \quad \text{(HM 2-73, 2-74)}, $$

normal gravity on the ellipsoid at the equator and the poles; $k = b\gamma_p/(a\gamma_e) - 1$; the gravity flattening $f^* = (\gamma_p - \gamma_e)/\gamma_e$; and the zonal coefficients of $V$ in spherical harmonics,

$$ J_2 = \frac{e^2}{3}\left(1 - \frac{2}{15}\,\frac{m e'}{q_0}\right), \qquad J_{2n} = (-1)^{n+1}\,\frac{3e^{2n}}{(2n+1)(2n+3)}\left(1 - n + 5n\,\frac{J_2}{e^2}\right) \quad \text{(HM 2-90, 2-92, 2-92′)}. $$

Given $J_2$ instead of $f$, as GRS 80 is, $e^2$ is the fixed point of $e^2 = 3J_2 + \frac{4}{15}\,\frac{\omega^2 a^3}{GM}\,\frac{e^3}{2q_0}$ (Moritz §3), reached by iteration from $e^2 = 3J_2$ in a few steps, since the map's derivative is of the order of $m$.

**Proposition 4 (Clairaut's theorem in closed form, HM 2-75).** $f + f^* = \dfrac{\omega^2 b}{\gamma_e}\left(1 + \dfrac{e' q_0'}{2q_0}\right)$.

*Proof.* With $A = e'q_0'/q_0$, $f + f^* = \gamma_p/\gamma_e - b/a = \left[\frac{GM}{a^2}\left(1 + \frac{mA}{3}\right) - \frac{GM}{a^2}\left(1 - m - \frac{mA}{6}\right)\right]/\gamma_e = \frac{GM\, m}{a^2}\left(1 + \frac{A}{2}\right)/\gamma_e$, and $GM\,m/a^2 = \omega^2 b$. ∎

The mean of normal gravity over the surface is $\bar\gamma = \int_0^{\pi/2} \gamma\, \cos\varphi\,(1 - e^2\sin^2\varphi)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi \big/ \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos\varphi\,(1 - e^2\sin^2\varphi)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi$ (Moritz §3; the weight is the area element $MN\cos\varphi$), computed by calx's adaptive Gauss–Kronrod rule.

## 5. Normal Gravity on and above the Ellipsoid

**Proposition 5 (NGA eqs. 4-5, 4-6).** The components of $\gamma = \nabla U$ along the unit vectors $e_u$ (outward) and $e_\beta$ (northward) are

$$ \gamma_u = -\frac{1}{w}\left[\frac{GM}{u^2 + E^2} + \frac{\omega^2 a^2 E}{u^2 + E^2}\,\frac{q'}{q_0}\left(\frac12\sin^2\beta - \frac16\right)\right] + \frac{1}{w}\,\omega^2 u\cos^2\beta, $$

$$ \gamma_\beta = \frac{1}{w}\,\frac{\omega^2 a^2}{\sqrt{u^2 + E^2}}\,\frac{q}{q_0}\sin\beta\cos\beta - \frac{1}{w}\,\omega^2\sqrt{u^2 + E^2}\,\sin\beta\cos\beta, $$

with $q$, $q'$ at $x = E/u$; and $|\gamma| = \sqrt{\gamma_u^2 + \gamma_\beta^2}$ (NGA eq. 4-4).

*Proof.* $\gamma_u = h_u^{-1}\,\partial U/\partial u$ and $\gamma_\beta = h_\beta^{-1}\,\partial U/\partial\beta$ in the orthogonal coordinates of §2. Differentiating Proposition 2 with $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\arctan\frac{E}{u} = -\frac{E}{u^2 + E^2}$ and $\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{\mathrm{d}u} = -\frac{E\, q'}{u^2 + E^2}$ gives both. The second follows from the definitions: with $x = E/u$, $\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{\mathrm{d}x} = \frac{2x^2 + 3}{x^2(1 + x^2)} - \frac{3\arctan x}{x^3} = \frac{q'}{1 + x^2}$, and $\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}u} = -\frac{E}{u^2}$. ∎

On the ellipsoid $\gamma_\beta = 0$ (the surface is level), and $|\gamma|$ is Somigliana's closed formula

$$ \gamma(\varphi) = \gamma_e\,\frac{1 + k\sin^2\varphi}{\sqrt{1 - e^2\sin^2\varphi}} \quad \text{(NGA eq. 4-1)} $$

at geodetic latitude $\varphi$. The vector in Earth-fixed axes is $\gamma = \gamma_u e_u + \gamma_\beta e_\beta$, with $e_u$ and $e_\beta$ the columns of NGA's $R_1$ (eq. 4-18); projected on the local frame of `geodesy::figure` at the point's geodetic coordinates it gives the component down the geodetic normal, $\gamma_h$ (NGA eq. 4-16), and the component north in the meridian, $\gamma_\varphi$ (eq. 4-23). NGA reaches the same components through spherical axes, $R_2 R_1$ (eqs. 4-17 to 4-20); the rotation is the same.

*Remark.* Above the ellipsoid the normal plumb line curves: the direction of $\gamma$ departs from the geodetic normal toward the equator, by 1.7″ at 10 km and 3.4″ at 20 km near 45° (NGA §4.3 bounds it by 4″ below 20 km), and $|\gamma|$ exceeds $\gamma_h$ by less than 2 × 10⁻⁹ m s⁻² there (NGA: less than 1 μGal).

**The series in height.** For a point near the ellipsoid NGA gives the second-order Taylor series

$$ \gamma_h \approx \gamma\left[1 - \frac{2}{a}\left(1 + f + m - 2f\sin^2\varphi\right) h + \frac{3}{a^2}\,h^2\right] \quad \text{(NGA eq. 4-3)}. $$

Against the closed field it errs by $1.0 \times 10^{-7}$ m s⁻² (10 μGal) at 1 km, $1.4 \times 10^{-6}$ at 20 km and $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ at 100 km, where it is no longer for use.

## 6. Validity

The field is that of the ellipsoid's exterior and is the reference of gravimetry there. Below the ellipsoid ($h < 0$: the Dead Sea's shore, the sea floor, a mine) the same formulas give the analytic continuation of the exterior field, which is the field of no particular mass distribution but is smooth through the surface, as the free-air reduction assumes. Normal gravity is the reference of the real field, not the real field: they differ by the gravity disturbance, of tens of mGal (10⁻⁴ m s⁻²) and up to a few hundred over mountain ranges and trenches.
