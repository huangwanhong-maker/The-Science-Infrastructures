# karyotype: The International Description of Chromosomes

*Status: draft (2026-11-01).* The description of chromosomes and their abnormalities by ISCN, the International System for Human Cytogenomic Nomenclature (ISCN 2024; ISCN 2020): karyotypes in the short and detailed systems, microarray (`arr`) and sequencing (`seq`) results, parsed by ISCN's grammar written once in BNF and read by morphe's BNF reader; the human ideogram's bands on GRCh38 and GRCh37 as a tree; every karyotype interpreted as chromosomes made of oriented segments of the normal ones, with its copy number at every band, its losses and gains from where to where, its fusions, and its detailed system written from its short; and results described, copy-number segments as `arr` and junctions as `seq` (ADR 0064). Serves use (reading and checking reports; describing the calls of the `structural` module), learning and research (the theory note [karyotypes.md](../theory/karyotypes.md)).

## 1. Concepts

- **A karyotype is a word, and so is each of its chromosomes.** ISCN's text is a language over bytes: its grammar ([`grammar.hpp`](include/genomics/karyotype/grammar.hpp)) decides every description and, when one is wrong, says where its longest viable prefix ends and what could have followed (`at column 9, after "46,XX,du": unexpected 'l'; expected 'p' (of 'dup')`). Each chromosome of the cell it describes is in turn a word over oriented segments of the normal chromosomes: der(22)t(9;22)(q34;q11.2) is 22pter→22q11.2·9q34→9qter. The short system names the operations; the detailed system writes the words; the interpretation is the map from the one to the other.
- **Rearrangements are operations on words.** A breakpoint cuts a chromosome into a centric piece and an acentric one; a translocation of n chromosomes joins each centric piece to the acentric piece of the chromosome before it; inversions reverse a factor, deletions remove one, duplications repeat one, insertions move one; a derivative applies its abnormalities in order. Copy number is the multiset of the elementary segments the words are made of; a rearrangement is balanced exactly when it keeps that multiset, and the breakpoints shared by the derivatives of one rearrangement are what make it so (theory §3–4).
- **A band is a word too.** Band names are words of digits whose prefix order is the hierarchy of the ideogram (1p3 ⊇ 1p36 ⊇ 1p36.3 ⊇ 1p36.33); a breakpoint written at any resolution is the run of finest bands its name begins, a point somewhere in it. Every loss, gain and junction carries that uncertainty as an interval: the representative extent, the inner one (certainly affected) and the outer one (possibly affected).
- **Editions.** ISCN 2020 lists the breakpoints within a chromosome from pter to qter, ISCN 2016 from the centromere outward; on the short arm this changes what a duplication or an insertion means. Texts are read in either edition and held in 2020's order, which ISCN 2024 keeps.

## 3. Dependencies

`morphe::formal` (the BNF reader and Earley's parser), `genomics::sequence` (`Problem`). The checks use `calx::verification`. The bands come from the data library: `python data/acquisition/fetch.py ncbi-ideogram` (NCBI's GRCh38 and GRCh37 ideograms at 400, 550 and 850 bands, UCSC's hg38 and hg19 cytoBand tables; [`data/registry/ncbi-ideogram.json`](../../../data/registry/ncbi-ideogram.json)). The fixtures are made by [`tests/fixtures/make_karyotype_fixtures.py`](../tests/fixtures/make_karyotype_fixtures.py).

## 5. Performance

The checks: `bands` 33, `grammar` 32, `interpret` 52, `cytogps` 33, `describe` 23 (173 in all, 2026-11-01). A karyotype of a few hundred characters is parsed by Earley's parser in well under a millisecond on the development machine and interpreted on the 850-band ideogram in about as long; the checks run in seconds.

## 7. Open Questions

1. **ish, nuc ish and rsa** are recognised and kept as text; their probes, signals and counts are not read. FISH results would need the probes' coordinates.
2. **Homologies and strands of sequencing results** (Ordulu et al.'s braces, 67,539,99{7–5}, and the strand marks of rearrangement sequences inside the detailed system) are not read; junctions with strands are read in their own form.
3. **The 300-band level** is not published by NCBI; texts at that resolution are read on the 400 level's prefixes, which hold them.
4. **Clinical regions.** ClinGen's dosage-sensitivity curation of regions and genes would name the recurrent regions a change overlaps; its licence and a manifest are planned.
5. **A tandem duplication inside another rearrangement.** The graph of junctions holds each piece once; a duplicated piece is recognised when its junction is a rearrangement of its own (it is grouped apart), not when it lies within a complex one, which is then written as cx.
6. **Breakpoints of different homologues in one band** are placed in the order written; their copy number in that band is that of one admissible placement (theory §5.3).
