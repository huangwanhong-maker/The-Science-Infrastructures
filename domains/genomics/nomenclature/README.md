# nomenclature: The Description of Sequence Variants

*Status: draft (2026-11-01).* The international description of how a genome departs from its reference: the HGVS Nomenclature (version 21.1; den Dunnen et al. 2016; Hart et al. 2024) read by its grammar and written back; a change described by the standard's rules with the canonical forms of VCF and of NCBI's SPDI; the projection of a genomic change through transcripts to c., n., r. and p.; the consequences of the Sequence Ontology in Ensembl VEP's order of severity; VCF's structural-variant and breakend records, as adjacencies and as HGVS descriptions of deletions, duplications, inversions, insertions, copy numbers and translocations (ADR 0064). Serves use (the Genome Workbench's descriptions; the structural module's calls described), learning and research (the theory note [nomenclature.md](../theory/nomenclature.md): the nomenclature as a formal language, its semantics, normal forms, and where the standard is ambiguous).

## 1. Concepts

- **A description is a word of a context-free language.** The grammar is written once, in BNF, from the standard's definitions and every one of its examples, and parsed by morphe's BNF reader on Earley's parser (`morphe/formal/bnf.hpp`); its named tree becomes a typed value, which the formatter writes back in the canonical form (three-letter amino acids with Ter; no deleted sequence after del). The grammar is unambiguous on every example (the checks count derivations), and its diagnostics come from the parser's chart: how far the text is right and what may follow.
- **A context-free grammar does not say everything.** Conditions of meaning (a substitution of one base, a range of two different positions written 5′ to 3′, an insertion between adjacent positions, variants in cis that touch being one delins) are checked on the value; a few faults (the 3′ rule, a position beyond the transcript) need the reference sequence.
- **A description denotes a haplotype.** An exact g. description is a change of the reference (`change_of`); two changes are equivalent when they make the same haplotype. An indel's class is an interval of placements; VCF takes its left end, HGVS its right end (the 3′ rule), SPDI its hull (Theorems 1 and 2 of the theory note, held to brute force).
- **The 3′ rule is the transcript's.** A change is projected onto a transcript by describing it on the strand the transcript reads, so a minus-strand gene's c. description takes the genomic left end; placed on the genome, the standard's exception at exon–exon junctions holds by itself.
- **The protein is predicted by translation.** The variant transcript is translated with the record's genetic code and compared with the reference protein, as the standard asks; frameshifts count their new stop from the first changed residue, extensions from the new residue.

## 3. Dependencies

`genomics::sequence` (codes, genetic codes, GenBank and its locations), `genomics::annotation` (GFF3), `genomics::variant` (Tan et al.'s normalisation, VCF records); `morphe::formal` (the BNF reader and Earley's parser). The checks use `calx::random` and `calx::verification`.

## 5. Performance

Measured on the development machine (i7-11370H, WSL, -O2), 2026-11-01: the grammar compiles in milliseconds; parsing the standard's 702 descriptions and 357 locations, with their derivations counted, takes about a second. Results worth knowing: the standard's 702 descriptions all parse, unambiguously, and write back to their own text; 92 of 92 single-variant answers of Mutalyzer's extractor reproduced; 352 of 352 c. positions of three MANE transcripts as VEP numbers them; 53 of 53 real variants in g./m., SPDI and VCF as Mutalyzer and NCBI write them, 46 of 46 c. projections as Mutalyzer's; 368 BRCA1 isoforms, HBB, CFTR and the 13 mitochondrial CDSs translated through their models as RefSeq translates them.

## 7. Open Questions

1. **The description extractor.** A delins is described here as one variant after trimming; the standard asks that changes separated by an unchanged base be described apart, and Mutalyzer's extractor (Vis et al. 2015) decomposes a delins into an allele of minimal variants (5_8delinsCTGC as [5A>C;6_8inv]). Its LCS-based algorithm belongs in morphe's `formal` and would be used here.
2. **Splicing and new starts.** A change at a splice site is p.?, an intronic one has no protein description; predicted splice effects (r.spl) and new upstream starts (p.Met1ext-5) need models of splicing and of translation initiation.
3. **NMD** (VEP's NMD_transcript_variant) and the incomplete terminal codon are not predicted.
4. **Protein repeats.** Changes in a protein's tandem repeats are written as del or dup; the standard prefers p.Ala2[9] "when the repeat is variable in the population", knowledge outside the sequence.
5. **Remote references.** Insertions of another reference's sequence (`ins[NC_000022.10:g.35788169_35788352]`) are parsed and written but have no haplotype without that reference; a resolver passed in by the caller would give them one.
6. **ISCN.** The derivative chromosome's centromere is passed in; the karyotype module's ideogram is the natural source, through the caller.
