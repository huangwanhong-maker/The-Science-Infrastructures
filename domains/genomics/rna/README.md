# rna: RNA Secondary Structure by Grammars

*Status: draft (2026-10-25).* The secondary structures of RNA and the stochastic context-free grammars that generate sequences with their structures: Nussinov's maximal pairing, Knudsen and Hein's grammar and a reversal-closed presentation of it, folding by the Viterbi parse, pair probabilities and maximum expected accuracy, and training from sequences alone by expectation maximisation, untied or tied along the orbits of a group acting on the grammar. Serves learning, use and research; the theory is [theory/rna-structure.md](../theory/rna-structure.md).

## 1. Concepts

- **A structure is a parse.** A secondary structure is a set of non-crossing pairs; a structure grammar derives each structure it allows by exactly one derivation, so that summing over derivations sums over structures. The grammars are morphe's (`formal/grammar.hpp`), and every computation on them is morphe's: the Viterbi structure is its best parse over (max, +), the probability of a sequence its inside value, the pair probabilities and the E-step its outside values and expected rule counts (`formal/inside_outside.hpp`), the symmetries its rule orbits (`formal/equivariant.hpp`). This module writes no inside–outside of its own.
- **Three choices make a grammar**, each explicit: the shape (Knudsen and Hein's lists built from the left, or lists built from both ends, which makes the rule set closed under reversal), lonely pairs (allowed, or every helix of two pairs or more), and the set of pairs that have rules (all sixteen, the six canonical ones, or their closure under a symmetry).
- **A symmetry is a tie.** A subgroup of the group of order 8 generated by the Klein group of the bases and reversal (`genomics::grammar`) that leaves the rule set invariant permutes the rules; tying the probabilities along its orbits gives a model whose sequences are exactly as probable as their images, and the tied M-step is the exact maximiser of the expected complete log-likelihood, so the tied EM never decreases the likelihood either. The likelihood-ratio test of the tie is the data's verdict on the symmetry.
- **One recursion for additive weights.** Nussinov's maximal pairing and the MEA structure are the same dynamic programme over non-crossing structures with different weights.

*Caveat.* The grammars are lightweight: no stacking energies, no loop lengths, no pseudoknots. Trained from sequences alone, they find what they can: on *E. coli*'s tRNAs the canonical grammar without lonely pairs learns the acceptor stem, and Knudsen and Hein's grammar as published, from the same start, does not (theory §6).

## 5. Performance

On the i7-11370H (eight threads for the E-step): training the canonical grammar without lonely pairs (42 binary rules) on 24 tRNAs (1,902 bases) to convergence in 2.4 s; each tie with its untied refit 6–8 s; the whole tRNA check 36 s. Every computation is O(n³ |R_binary|) per sequence; probabilities are doubles and underflow beyond about 350 bases (refused).

## 7. Open Questions

1. **Long RNAs.** morphe's inside–outside computes in a semiring of doubles, which underflow beyond about 350 bases; a scaled or logarithmic inside–outside belongs in morphe, and would carry the grammars to ribosomal RNAs.
2. **The recursion for additive weights** names no object in nature (it maximises over non-crossing partial matchings); when a second domain needs it, it moves to morphe's combinatorics.
3. **Richer grammars.** Stacking, loop lengths and the grammars of Dowell and Eddy beyond G6 would make better structures; trained from structures they need reference structures from a database, which the data library does not yet fetch.
4. **EM's local maxima.** The fit depends on its start (the untied fit started from the tied one is better than the one from the uniform start); restarts, or annealing, would make the verdicts firmer.
