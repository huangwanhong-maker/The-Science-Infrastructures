*Research notes, 2026-11-04. ## 1. Surfaces from Volumes: Marching Cubes and Its Topological Repairs, Surface Nets, Dual Contouring, Smoothing without Shrinkage, Level Sets, and Partial Volume

### Takeaway
A watertight lumen surface is usually extracted as an isosurface: of the probability map or of a level-set function (marching cubes with a topologically consistent case table, or flying edges), or of a binary label map by (constrained) surface nets, which avoid the staircase. It is then smoothed with a non-shrinking filter (Taubin λ|μ or windowed-sinc) and remeshed. Partial volume is handled by placing the surface at an interior iso-level (the probability 0.5, or the gradient maximum in level-set methods) rather than at voxel faces. The challenges show that segmentation choices still dominate the variability of geometry, not the surface algorithm.

### Inferences
- For a probabilistic segmentation, the natural surface is the 0.5 isosurface of the (smoothly interpolated) probability field by MC33 or flying edges. For a hard binary label, surface nets (constrained) or Whitaker-style anti-aliasing followed by MC avoid the staircase without moving the surface by more than about half a voxel. That bound follows from Gibson's cube constraint; it is an inference from the method's design, not a measured figure.
- Topological guarantees of MC33 hold for the trilinear interpolant. They do not repair topological errors in the segmentation itself (spurious bridges between adjacent vessels, broken thin vessels). Those must be fixed upstream (topology-aware losses, §3) or by explicit editing.
- Non-shrinking smoothing (Taubin λ|μ) matters for small vessels. Plain Laplacian smoothing shrinks the radius, and a 1-voxel shrink is a large relative error at 2–4 voxels across (compare Hoogeveen's ≥3-pixel limit, §5).

## 2. Centrelines and Skeletons: Medial Axis, Voronoi Centrelines (VMTK), Topological Thinning, Distance Transforms, Minimal Paths, Pruning, and Radius by Maximal Inscribed Spheres

### Takeaway
Three families coexist. (i) Voxel thinning (Lee–Kashyap–Chu 1994; Palágyi–Kuba; Bertrand–Couprie isthmus thinning) is topology-preserving by construction, fast, and standard for large graphs, but noisy and in need of pruning. (ii) Voronoi or medial-axis centrelines on a surface (Antiga 2003; VMTK) are minimal paths of an Eikonal equation on the Voronoi diagram weighted by the inscribed-sphere radius, so that paths follow the largest spheres (the exact speed or cost convention is noted below). They carry the maximal-inscribed-sphere radius exactly but need watertight, reasonably smooth surfaces and labelled endpoints. (iii) Minimal paths and trackers on the image (Deschamps–Cohen; ridge traversal; CNN trackers) need no prior segmentation. Radius from inscribed spheres is reliable only when the lumen spans several voxels: at least 3 pixels across for diameter (Hoogeveen 1998), and 5–6 for flow.

**Classical: Voronoi centrelines and VMTK**
- Antiga et al. (2003) introduced geometric analysis by "solving Eikonal equation on Voronoi diagram". - VMTK's tutorial defines centrelines as "weighted shortest paths traced between two extremal points" that run on the Voronoi diagram, "the place where the centers of maximal inscribed spheres are defined". Each centreline point carries its sphere's radius in the array `MaximumInscribedSphereRadius`. Centrelines end at the poles of the seeds, not at the seeds themselves (`-endpoints 1` appends the end segments). The Voronoi diagram is "very noisy", but its hair-like structures "do not affect our centerlines". ### Inferences
- In a C++ implementation: for watertight, smoothed surfaces of arterial trees (CFD-scale vessels, at least 3 voxels across), the Voronoi or Eikonal centreline (Antiga 2003) gives centring and MIS radius directly. For huge microvascular volumes, voxel thinning plus distance-transform radius is the only scalable route (cf. §3). For noisy ML surfaces, solving the Eikonal equation on the mesh (Sanchez Van Moer & Shadden 2026) is a recent, more robust alternative.
- Spurious branches: thinning yields spurs wherever the boundary is rough. The usual pruning is by branch length against the local radius, i.e. drop a terminal branch shorter than k·R at its junction. This rule is common practice but was not sourced here (gap). Szymczak et al. (2006) prune short branches explicitly.
- The radius from an inscribed sphere measures the largest ball, i.e. the *minimum* half-width of a non-circular lumen. Where cross-sections are elliptical (stenoses, veins), the equivalent-area radius differs systematically; this follows from the definition.
- Quantisation: with a surface accurate to about ±0.5 voxel, the relative error of R is about 0.5/R_vox, i.e. 25% at R = 2 voxels and 10% at R = 5. This is consistent in magnitude with Hoogeveen's ≥3-pixel diameter criterion. It is an inference, not a measured figure.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that directly measures maximal-inscribed-sphere radius error against voxel size on a phantom; [the extended search](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10933244/) turned up only related small-lumen PC-MRI phantom work and a NIST sphere-metrology note (about 2% error with ≥5 voxels across a sphere, report not read; **[unverified]**).
- A quantitative comparison of thinning algorithms (Lee 1994 against Palágyi–Kuba against isthmus thinning) on vascular data, including spur counts, was not found in this pass.

## 3. Vascular Graphs: Extraction and Benchmarks, Graph Features, Murray's Law, Anastomoses and Betti Numbers

### Takeaway
A vascular graph is a skeleton, with junction voxels collapsed to nodes and branches to edges carrying length, radius, tortuosity and orientation. Whole-brain mouse graphs have more than 10⁸ segments (Kirst 2020), and VesselGraph (2021) is the standard benchmark for learning on them. The cortical microvasculature is a loopy network (high first Betti number), not a tree (Blinder 2013). Murray's cube law holds roughly in some territories (cerebral arteries, n ≈ 2.9 ± 0.7). Pooled human and mammalian coronary data, however, give an exponent of about 2.4 (meta-analysis 2024), and pulmonary arteries about 2.3. Graph topology is now evaluated with Betti-number errors and Betti matching, and enforced with clDice and topological losses.

### Inferences
- For a graph-based C++ module, the first Betti number of the vessel graph (β₁ = E − V + C for a graph with C components) counts independent loops (anastomoses). The Circle of Willis is ideally a single loop, and cortical networks have many. Comparing β₀ and β₁ of the graph with those of the segmentation (by cubical homology) detects topological errors introduced by skeletonisation; TopCoW and the Betti-matching literature evaluate segmentations on exactly these quantities.
- Murray's exponent should be *estimated*, not assumed, in any radius-assignment or radius-repair step. The evidence (2.9 in cerebral arteries; about 2.15–2.4 in human coronaries; about 2.3 in pulmonary arteries) means a fixed n = 3 builds in bias.
- Radius errors feed the exponent fits: with r measured to ±0.5 voxel (§2), small-branch radii, and so the fitted exponents, are noisiest. Exponent estimates should be restricted to branches several voxels across.

### Gaps
- Kirst et al. (2020) and Paetzold et al. (2021) describe their skeletonisation (which thinning variant, how spurs are pruned) in their methods, which were not read here.
- No source was found in this pass for standard spur-pruning thresholds, or for a benchmark of graph-extraction accuracy (node and edge F1 against manual graphs) beyond the TopCoW component-detection metric and VesselGraph's link-prediction tasks.
- The bifurcation-angle optimality tests (Zamir; Uylings) were not verified; Rossitti & Löfgren 1993 found no clear correlation between measured and theoretical optimal angles.

## 5. Imaging Physics and Geometry of Vascular Imaging: CTA, MRA (TOF, PC, CE), DICOM Patient Coordinates (LPS), NIfTI qform and sform (RAS), Voxel Sizes against Vessel Calibres, and the Limits of Resolution

### Takeaway
CTA gives an iodinated lumen at more than 250 HU, sampled with slices under 1 mm (0.5–0.6 mm at best; photon-counting CT reaches 0.2 mm). TOF MRA needs inflow and suffers saturation; PC MRA encodes velocity, and 4D flow runs at 1.5–3 mm isotropic. 7T TOF reaches 0.15 mm isotropic with motion correction. DICOM stores the centre of the first voxel and the row and column direction cosines in LPS. NIfTI stores RAS through qform (quaternion with qfac) or sform (general affine), so converting between them negates x and y. Diameters are reliable only at 3 or more voxels across, and flow at 5–6 or more.

**DICOM patient coordinates (PS3.3 C.7.6.2, Image Plane Module)**
- Image Position (Patient) (0020,0032): "the x, y, and z coordinates of the upper left hand corner (center of the first voxel transmitted) of the image, in mm". Image Orientation (Patient) (0020,0037): "the direction cosines of the first row and the first column with respect to the patient", ordered as row x, y, z, then column x, y, z. Pixel Spacing (0028,0030): "adjacent row spacing (delimiter) adjacent column spacing in mm" — [DICOM PS3.3 §C.7.6.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.6.2.html)
- Patient axes (bipeds): "x-axis is increasing to the left hand side of the patient", "y-axis is increasing to the posterior side", "z-axis is increasing toward the head". The system is right-handed, i.e. LPS — [DICOM PS3.3 §C.7.6.2.1.1](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.6.2.html)
- Equation C.7.6.2.1-1 maps the integer pixel (i, j) to patient coordinates P using S (Image Position), X (row direction cosine), Y (column direction cosine), i ("Column integer index… first column is index zero"), Δi ("Column pixel resolution of Pixel Spacing"), j (row index) and Δj ("Row pixel resolution"). The direction cosines must be orthonormal. Equation C.7.6.2.1-2 uses sub-pixel (c, r) with the *edge* of the first pixel at zero — [DICOM PS3.3 §C.7.6.2.1.1](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.6.2.html)
- Slice Thickness (0018,0050) is "nominal". Spacing Between Slices (0018,0088) is measured "center-to-center" and shall not be negative except where an IOD defines its sign. Slice Location (0020,1041) is "relative to an unspecified implementation specific reference point" — [DICOM PS3.3 §C.7.6.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.6.2.html)

**NIfTI-1 (`nifti1.h`)**
- Method 1 (qform_code = 0): x = pixdim[1]·i and so on, "No particular spatial orientation"; kept for ANALYZE compatibility. Method 2 (qform_code > 0, "the 'normal' case"): [x y z]ᵀ = R·[pixdim1·i, pixdim2·j, qfac·pixdim3·k]ᵀ + qoffset, with R a proper rotation from the quaternion and qfac = ±1 stored in pixdim[0] (0 is taken as 1); intended for scanner-anatomical coordinates from DICOM (0020,0032), (0020,0037), (0028,0030) and (0018,0050). Method 3 (sform_code > 0): a general affine via srow_x, srow_y, srow_z, which "makes no use of pixdim[]". The centre of voxel (0,0,0) maps to qoffset — [nifti1.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NIFTI-Imaging/nifti_clib/master/niftilib/nifti1.h)
- NIfTI's frame is RAS: "+x is Right, +y is Anterior, +z is Superior", while "DICOM +Xd is Left, +Yd is Posterior, +Zd is Superior". Hence qoffset_x = −px, qoffset_y = −py, qoffset_z = pz, and the first two columns of R are [−xa −ya; −xb −yb; xc yc] from the Image Orientation cosines: "To transform between DICOM and NIFTI, you just have to negate the x- and y-coordinates". The third column's sign is inferred from successive slices' Image Position, a method that "occasionally fails" (R. W. Cox) — [nifti1.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NIFTI-Imaging/nifti_clib/master/niftilib/nifti1.h)
- xform codes: NIFTI_XFORM_UNKNOWN 0, SCANNER_ANAT 1, ALIGNED_ANAT 2, TALAIRACH 3, MNI_152 4, TEMPLATE_OTHER 5 — [nifti1.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NIFTI-Imaging/nifti_clib/master/niftilib/nifti1.h)

### Inferences
- **Pixel-spacing trap.** From the DICOM definitions above, stepping along a row (column index i increasing, direction X = IOP[0:3]) uses the *column* spacing Δi, which is the *second* value of Pixel Spacing. Stepping down a column (j, direction Y = IOP[3:6]) uses the row spacing, the first value. A C++ reader should therefore build the voxel-to-LPS affine as A = [X·PS[1], Y·PS[0], N·Δk | IPP], with N the slice direction. N and Δk should be derived from the IPPs of successive slices (projected on X × Y), not from Slice Thickness or Slice Location, which are nominal or implementation-specific. Toward NIfTI, left-multiply by diag(−1, −1, 1).
- The infrastructure's conventions require frames to be explicit. A vessel model should therefore carry its frame (LPS from DICOM, or RAS from NIfTI), its units (mm in both; SI metres internally per the repository's rules) and the sform or qform code it came from, with the DICOM→NIfTI flip as an explicit transformation.
- Resolution against calibre: with CTA at about 0.5 mm (conventional) or 0.2 mm (photon-counting), 3 voxels across means vessels of about 1.5 mm or 0.6 mm diameter at best for reliable diameters. 4D flow at 1.5–3 mm with 5–6 voxels across restricts accurate flow to vessels of roughly 8–18 mm, i.e. the aorta and great vessels, and makes intracranial 4D-flow radii from the same data unreliable. 7T TOF at 0.15 mm reaches roughly 0.45 mm vessels, the order of lenticulostriate arteries. These are arithmetic consequences of the cited criteria.

### Gaps
- Typical vessel calibres (aorta about 20–30 mm, carotid about 6–8 mm, MCA about 2–3 mm, proximal coronaries about 2–4 mm, lenticulostriate arteries a few hundred μm) are commonly quoted but were **not verified** against a primary anatomical source in this pass.
- The Bolus-tracking trigger threshold in HU is not specified numerically in the SCCT guideline (it says only "below the target attenuation"); vendor and institutional values (often quoted as 100–150 HU) are **[unverified]**.
- The precedence of sform over qform when both are set is described in `nifti1.h` "WHY 3 METHODS?", which was not read in full; NIfTI-2 differences were not covered.
- DICOM's enhanced multi-frame objects (Plane Position and Orientation functional groups), and the patient-position effects (HFS, FFS) on the LPS mapping for non-axial acquisitions, were not covered.
- The equations C.7.6.2.1-1 and -2 themselves were not rendered in the fetched page; their variable definitions were quoted.
