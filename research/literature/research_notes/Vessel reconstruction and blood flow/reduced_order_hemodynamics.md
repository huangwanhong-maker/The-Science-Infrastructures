## 1. 0D (lumped-parameter) models: Windkessels, the lumped heart, closed loops, Poiseuille–Kirchhoff networks, Murray's law

### Takeaway
0D models are ODE/DAE systems on an electrical-analogue graph: the 2-, 3- and 4-element Windkessels (Frank 1899; Westerhof 1969; Stergiopulos 1999; reviewed by Westerhof, Lankhaar & Westerhof 2009) close the network's outlets, a time-varying elastance (Suga & Sagawa 1973/1974) drives it, and closed-loop circulations (reviewed by Shi, Lawford & Hose 2011) join heart, arteries, veins and pulmonary circuit. Steady Poiseuille resistance networks are exactly a weighted graph Laplacian (Kirchhoff) problem, and Murray's minimum-work law (1926) gives the r³ branching rule against which image-derived radii can be checked.

### Cited Findings
**Classical**
- Otto Frank's 1899 paper "Die Grundform des arteriellen Pulses" (Z Biol 37:483–526; no DOI) is the origin of the Windkessel; an English translation was published in 1990 as "The basic shape of the arterial pulse. First treatise: Mathematical analysis", J Mol Cell Cardiol 22(3):255–277, with a translator's note by K. Sagawa, ibid. 22(3):253–254 — [Frank 1990 transl.](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-2828(90)91460-O); [Sagawa 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-2828(90)91459-K)
- The standard review of the arterial Windkessel (2-element RC, 3-element with characteristic impedance, 4-element with inertance) is Westerhof, Lankhaar & Westerhof 2009 — [Westerhof et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11517-008-0359-2)
- The 3-element "Westerhof" model descends from analog studies of the systemic arterial tree (Westerhof, Bosman, De Vries & Noordergraaf 1969) — [Westerhof et al. 1969](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9290(69)90024-4)
- Total arterial inertance was introduced as the fourth element of the Windkessel by Stergiopulos, Westerhof & Westerhof 1999 — [Stergiopulos et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.1999.276.1.H81)
- A 3-element Windkessel used as a 1D outlet condition is written (openBF) as Q(1 + R1/R2) + C R1 dQ/dt = (P − Pout)/R2 + C dP/dt, i.e. with Pc the pressure across C: C dPc/dt = Q − (Pc − Pout)/R2; openBF solves it at the 0D/1D interface by Newton iteration on the interface area and velocity — [Benemerito et al. 2024, eq. 2](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/ad9663)
- Time-varying elastance: Suga & Sagawa 1974 showed the instantaneous pressure–volume ratio of the excised, supported canine left ventricle, E(t) = P(t)/(V(t) − V0), and Suga, Sagawa & Shoukas 1973 its load independence and its change with epinephrine and heart rate — [Suga & Sagawa 1974](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.RES.35.1.117); [Suga, Sagawa & Shoukas 1973](https://doi.org/10.1161/01.RES.32.3.314)
- A widely used normalised elastance waveform comes from Stergiopulos, Meister & Westerhof 1996 (determinants of stroke volume and aortic pressures) — [Stergiopulos et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.1996.270.6.H2050) (the "double-Hill" form attributed to this paper is from memory **[unverified detail]**)
- The review of 0D and 1D cardiovascular models (heart, valves, vasculature; pressure-dependent vessel properties; closed loops) is Shi, Lawford & Hose 2011, BioMed Eng OnLine 10:33 — [Shi et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-925X-10-33)
- Representative closed-loop 0D circulations: a minimal model with ventricular interaction and valve dynamics (Smith et al. 2004); orthostatic stress (Heldt et al. 2002); carotid baroregulation with a pulsating heart (Ursino 1998); a dimensionally-heterogeneous closed loop (Blanco & Feijóo 2013); a multiscale model with aortic valvular and arterial stenoses (Liang et al. 2009) — [Smith et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.medengphy.2003.10.001); [Heldt et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.00241.2001); [Ursino 1998](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.1998.275.5.H1733); [Blanco & Feijóo 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.medengphy.2012.07.011); [Liang et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11517-009-0449-9)
- Valves for both 0D and 1D models: Mynard, Davidson, Penny & Smolich 2012, "A simple, versatile valve model for use in lumped parameter and one-dimensional cardiovascular models" — [Mynard et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/cnm.1466)
- The relation of lumped models to 1D models (a 0D compartment as the spatial average of a 1D segment) is analysed by Milišić & Quarteroni 2004; nonlinear lumped-parameter models for networks by Ghitti, Toro & Müller 2022 — [Milišić & Quarteroni 2004](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an:2004036); [Ghitti et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2022052)
- The CellML/OpenCOR "roadmap for cardiovascular circulation model" (Safaei et al. 2016) and a bond-graph model of the cerebral circulation (Safaei et al. 2018) are the CellML-based 0D/1D lineage — [Safaei et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1113/JP272660); [Safaei et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2018.00148)
- Textbook treatment of lumped models in physiology: Ottesen, Olufsen & Larsen 2004, *Applied Mathematical Models in Human Physiology* (SIAM) — [Ottesen et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9780898718287)

**2023–2026**
- svZeroDSolver (Menon et al. 2025, JOSS 10(109):7595): a C++ lumped-parameter solver, models defined as a JSON dictionary of "blocks" (vessels, junctions, boundary conditions), with Python API and C++ shared library and a calibrator that fits 0D vessel parameters to flow and pressure — [Menon et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.07595); [JOSS page](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.07595)
- Richter et al. 2025 calibrate high-dimensional Windkessel posteriors by Sequential Monte Carlo on a 0D surrogate optimised from a single 3D run; optimising the 0D model a priori lowered its median approximation error by nearly an order of magnitude over 72 public vascular models — [Richter et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2024.0223)
- Fevola, Bradde, Triverio & Grivet-Talocia 2023 estimate Windkessel and higher-order lumped outlet conditions from pressure and flow by time-domain vector fitting, tested on a 55-artery 1D model — [Fevola et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13239-023-00669-z)

### Inferences
- For a C++ 0D solver, the natural representation is a graph of blocks with element equations assembled into one DAE (as svZeroDSolver does with JSON blocks); RCR, RCRL and coronary/structured-tree outlets are then blocks, and the heart is a time-varying capacitor E(t).
- Steady Poiseuille flow on a vascular graph with conductances g_e = πr_e⁴/(8μL_e) is a weighted graph-Laplacian system L p = s (Kirchhoff's current law at nodes); this is a standard result (not tied to one source here) and is a direct verification target: compare the solver's steady limit with the Laplacian solve, and compare the image-derived radii with Murray's r³ rule (Σ r_children³ = r_parent³) as a sanity check of segmentation.
- For microvessels from images, a constant viscosity is wrong by factors depending on diameter and haematocrit; Pries et al. 1994 is the standard correction.

## 2. 1D pulse-wave models: governing equations, tube laws, numerics, junctions and boundary conditions, key network models

### Takeaway
The 1D model is a 2×2 hyperbolic balance law for area A and flow Q (or velocity U) on each edge, closed by a tube law P(A) (elastic √A law, optionally Kelvin–Voigt viscoelastic), with junctions imposing mass conservation and continuity of total (or static) pressure through the characteristics, and outlets closed by Windkessels or structured trees. The canonical numerical families are MacCormack/Lax–Wendroff finite differences, Taylor–Galerkin and locally conservative Galerkin finite elements, spectral/hp discontinuous Galerkin, MUSCL/ADER finite volumes and the method of characteristics; Boileau et al. 2015 showed six such schemes agree closely on the same cases.

### Cited Findings
**Governing equations and tube laws**
- Formaggia, Lamponi & Quarteroni 2003 derive the 1D model by section-averaging the Navier–Stokes equations in a compliant tube (J Eng Math 47:251–276); Sherwin, Franke, Peiró & Parker 2003 write it in space-time variables for a vascular network (J Eng Math 47:217–250); Sherwin, Formaggia, Peiró & Franke 2003 treat variable mechanical properties and wave propagation in the human arterial system (Int J Numer Meth Fluids 43:673–700) — [Formaggia et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:ENGI.0000007980.01347.29); [Sherwin et al. 2003a](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:ENGI.0000007979.32871.e2); [Sherwin et al. 2003b](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.543)
- Earlier formulations: Barnard et al. 1966 (fluid flow in compliant tubes) and Hughes & Lubliner 1973 (1D theory in the larger vessels) — [Barnard et al. 1966](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0006-3495(66)86690-0); [Hughes & Lubliner 1973](https://doi.org/10.1016/0025-5564(73)90027-8)
- A current statement of the system (openBF): ∂A/∂t + ∂Q/∂z = 0; ∂Q/∂t + ∂(αQ²/A)/∂z + (A/ρ)∂P/∂z = −2μ(γ+2)/ρ · Q/A; P(A) = Pext + (β/A0)(√A − √A0) − (Γ/(A0√A)) ∂Q/∂z, the last term a Kelvin–Voigt wall viscosity (Γ = 0 gives the elastic case); γ sets the velocity profile and hence friction — [Benemerito et al. 2024, eq. 1](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/ad9663)
- The Boileau benchmark uses the elastic law referenced to diastole, P = Pext + Pd + (β/Ad)(√A − √Ad), with Pd and Ad the diastolic pressure and area; a scheme using A0 at zero transmural pressure instead gives slightly different wave speed and characteristic resistance, a source of small discrepancies when comparing — [Kim & Tithof 2025 (arXiv preprint), §8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18484)
- The Charlton et al. 2019 database model uses Voigt-type viscoelastic walls, laminar incompressible Newtonian blood (ρ = 1060 kg m⁻³, μ = 2.5 mPa s), a parabolic (Poiseuille-type) profile and no energy loss at bifurcations; the diastolic wave speed is c_d = √(2Eh/(3ρR_d)) and wall viscosity Γ = b1/(2R_d) + b0 with b1 = 150 g cm s⁻¹ and b0 = 600 g s⁻¹ (after Mynard & Smolich 2015) — [Charlton et al. 2019, Methods/Appendix](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.00218.2019) (the square root is reconstructed from extracted text; check eq. 3 of the paper)
- Friction and wall viscosity choices were tested against experiments by Wang et al. 2016; an approximate velocity profile function for large vessels was proposed by Bessems, Rutten & van de Vosse 2007; augmented 1D FSI systems for viscoelastic vessels by Bertaglia, Caleffi & Valiani 2020 and in networks by Piccioli et al. 2022 (IMEX Runge–Kutta finite volumes, with viscoelastic aortic bifurcation and the AN37 and ADAN56 networks) — [Wang et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2016.01.010); [Bessems et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112007005344); [Bertaglia et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2019.112772); [Piccioli et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2022.111364); [Piccioli et al. arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12614)

### Inferences
- For a C++ solver on an image-derived graph, the elastic √A tube law gives exact Riemann invariants W± = U ± 4c (standard for this law; not re-derived here), which make junction and Windkessel coupling a small nonlinear system per node solved by Newton — the approach openBF describes for its outlets.
- Image-derived segments are tapered; Charlton's model uses linear taper and openBF attributes its largest discrepancies against the Charlton database to the absence of tapering in heavily tapered vessels — [Benemerito et al. 2024 §4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/ad9663). A C++ solver should therefore support A0(z) and β(z), which needs a well-balanced scheme (Müller et al. 2013; Delestre & Lagrée 2013).
- A sensible first implementation is second-order MUSCL FV (simplest to verify and the method of openBF) or a DG scheme (Sherwin 2003 lineage), with the Boileau cases as regression tests.

### Gaps
- Alastruey, Parker & Sherwin 2012 ("Arterial pulse wave haemodynamics", Pressure Surges conference) has no DOI; its bibliographic details are recalled, not verified.
- Formaggia, Lamponi & Quarteroni 2003 is widely cited for a Taylor–Galerkin scheme and Olufsen 2000 for a two-step Lax–Wendroff scheme; I did not confirm the schemes from the full texts in this session **[unverified]**.

## 3. Benchmarks with reference data, and analytic test solutions

### Takeaway
The verification ladder for a 1D code is: analytic (Poiseuille; Womersley 1955; exact Riemann solutions for the √A and collapsible tube laws; the reflection-free pulse of Boileau case 1) → Boileau et al. 2015's six cases (theory, 3D, in vitro 37-artery network, ADAN56) → 1D-vs-3D comparisons (Xiao, Alastruey & Figueroa 2014; Jin & Alastruey 2021) → in vivo and population data (Reymond 2009; Charlton 2019 PWDB, public-domain on Zenodo). Boileau's own abstract says all inputs, experimental data and every scheme's solutions are online, but I could not locate the official URL in this session; openBF's repository carries reference solutions for three of the cases.

### Cited Findings
**Boileau et al. 2015 (six cases, six schemes)**
- Six schemes (DG, LCG, Galerkin least-squares FE, FV, MacCormack FD, simplified trapezium rule) on six cases of increasing complexity; accuracy assessed against theoretical results, 3D numerical data in compatible distensible domains, or experiments in a silicone-tube network; "All the information used in this study, including the input data for all benchmark cases, experimental data where available and numerical solutions for each scheme, is made publicly available online" — [Boileau et al. 2015 abstract via Europe PMC](https://doi.org/10.1002/cnm.2732)
- The cases, as reconstructed from secondary sources: (1) a reflection-free tube with a theoretical solution, inviscid and viscous, single pulse in the linear regime [search summaries of the paper: [Swansea record](https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa22154), [bohrium summary](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/a-benchmark-study-of-numerical-schemes-for-one-dimensional-arterial-blood-flow-modelling/814619220758233090-4094)]; (2) common carotid artery and (3) upper thoracic aorta, single vessels against 3D; (4) aortic (iliac) bifurcation against 3D — [Benemerito et al. 2024 Tables 2–4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/ad9663); (5) the 37-artery in vitro silicone network (AN37) against experiment; (6) ADAN56 (56 arteries), compared among schemes — [Piccioli et al. 2022 §4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12614). The exact numbering and the parameters of case 1 are **[unverified]**: the full text could not be retrieved.
- Single-vessel and bifurcation parameters as reproduced in openBF: thoracic aorta L = 24.137 cm, R0 = 9.87 mm, h0 = 0.82 mm, E = 400 kPa; common carotid L = 12.6 cm, R0 = 2.6485 mm, h0 = 0.24 mm, E = 700 kPa; iliac bifurcation parent L = 8.6 cm, R0 = 7.581 mm, h0 = 0.9 mm, E = 500 kPa, daughters L = 8.5 cm, R0 = 5.492 mm, h0 = 0.68 mm, E = 700 kPa; each outlet an RCR Windkessel (values in the same tables) — [Benemerito et al. 2024 Tables 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/ad9663). Caveat: the Windkessel units printed in those tables look inconsistent between rows; take R1, R2, C from machine-readable files, not the table.
- Accuracy reported for Boileau's schemes against 3D (CCA, thoracic aorta, aortic bifurcation): upper bounds of relative error 1.2% pressure, 2.6% flow, 4.3% area; against the in vitro 37-artery network: 4.0% pressure, 25.6% flow — [Jin & Alastruey 2021, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2020.0881)
- openBF against Boileau: errors within 7% of the 1D reference; against 3D, main features captured but the maximum ΔP in the upper thoracic aorta underestimated by 35% (attributed to straight-tube and small-displacement assumptions); bifurcation within 10% of 3D; ADAN56 pressure waveforms within 8% across the tree, with larger differences in the internal carotid (a maximum flow-rate error of 39% is reported); error metrics are relative RMS, maximum, systolic and diastolic errors in P and Q — [Benemerito et al. 2024 §3.4, §4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6579/ad9663)
- Where the data are in practice: the openBF repository's `test/` directory holds reference files `ref_cca.jls`, `ref_ibif.jls`, `ref_adan56.jls` (and `ref_circle_of_willis.jls`) in Julia serialisation format — [openBF test directory](https://github.com/INSIGNEO/openBF/tree/master/test); a third-party project (hemo1d) states that it took Boileau's geometry, wall parameters, Windkessel constants and inflow waveforms from the model files distributed with openBF — [hemo1d](https://github.com/cmorregof/hemo1d) (search-result summary; repository not opened)
- A 2025 review digitised Boileau's carotid and bifurcation plots by hand for its verification, which suggests the original files are not easy to obtain — [Kim & Tithof 2025 §8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18484)

**1D versus 3D and in vitro**
- Xiao, Alastruey & Figueroa 2014: idealised compliant models of the common carotid, thoracic aorta, aortic bifurcation and the full aorta from arch to iliac bifurcation, with identical inflow/outflow conditions and compatible material laws, plus an iterative algorithm that uses 1D theory to choose outflow parameters for target systolic/diastolic pressures; agreement is good, especially in diastole — [Xiao et al. 2014 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1002/cnm.2598); error upper bounds 1.4% P, 2.1% Q, 2.6% A for the three simple models and 2.1% P, 4.9% Q for the 20-artery aorta — [Jin & Alastruey 2021, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2020.0881)
- In vitro 37-artery silicone network: Matthys et al. 2007 (elastic 1D vs measurement) and Alastruey et al. 2011 (visco-elastic 1D; upper bounds 2.5% P, 10.8% Q) — [Matthys et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2007.05.027); [Alastruey et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2011.05.041); [Jin & Alastruey 2021 Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2020.0881)
- Jin & Alastruey 2021 assess 1D against 3D and in vitro data across stenoses and aneurysms; their data are in the electronic supplementary material — [Jin & Alastruey 2021](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2020.0881); [PMC8086929](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8086929)
- Subject-specific aortic multiscale models and the effect of modelling assumptions: Alastruey et al. 2016; 1D vs 3D for fractional flow reserve: Blanco et al. 2018 — [Alastruey et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2016.0073); [Blanco et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-35344-0)
- Reymond et al. 2009 validated against in vivo MRI, Doppler and tonometry (upper bounds 6.0% P, 11.0% Q per Jin & Alastruey's table) — [Reymond et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.00037.2009); [Jin & Alastruey 2021](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2020.0881)

**Population database**
- Charlton et al. 2019 Pulse Wave Database (PWDB): 4,374 virtual healthy adults aged 25–75 (six age groups; a baseline per group and 728 variations each), with U, A, P and PPG waves at many sites; a 116-segment arterial network adapted from Mynard & Smolich 2015 (areas scaled by 1.5; hand arteries 97–116 added), periodic aortic inflow and 3-element Windkessels — [Charlton et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.00218.2019); [Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.3275625](https://zenodo.org/records/3275625)
- PWDB licence on Zenodo: ODC-PDDL (Open Data Commons Public Domain Dedication and License), version 0.2.0 — [Zenodo API record 3275625](https://zenodo.org/api/records/3275625). Its code repository is MIT, except PulseAnalyse10.m (GPL-3.0) and TTAlgorithm (own licence) — [pwdb LICENSE](https://github.com/peterhcharlton/pwdb/blob/master/LICENSE)
- A database of virtual healthy subjects for pulse wave velocity: Willemet, Chowienczyk & Alastruey 2015 — [Willemet et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.00175.2015)
- The Vascular Model Repository (Wilson, Ortiz & Johnson 2013) supplies image-derived geometries with simulation results; Pfaller et al. 2022 used N = 72 of its models to test automatically generated 0D and 1D models against rigid-wall 3D — [Wilson et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4025983); [Pfaller et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/cnm.3639)

**Analytic solutions**
- Womersley 1955 gives velocity, flow and viscous drag in a rigid tube for an oscillatory pressure gradient (Bessel-function solution) — [Womersley 1955](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1955.sp005276)
- Exact Riemann solutions: collapsible tubes with discontinuous mechanical properties (Toro & Siviglia 2013); veins including collapse (Spiller et al. 2017); the 1D blood flow equations with a general constant momentum-correction coefficient (Spilimbergo, Toro & Müller 2024) — [Toro & Siviglia 2013](https://doi.org/10.4208/cicp.210611.240212a); [Spiller et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amc.2017.01.024); [Spilimbergo et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.4208/cicp.OA-2023-0250)

### Inferences
- A practical verification suite for a C++ code: (a) steady Poiseuille/Kirchhoff on a graph (exact); (b) Womersley in the linear, rigid limit; (c) exact Riemann problems (Toro & Siviglia; Spiller; Spilimbergo) for shock/rarefaction structure and for venous collapse; (d) Boileau CCA, thoracic aorta, iliac bifurcation and ADAN56 via openBF's reference files; (e) AN37 against the published in vitro data (Matthys 2007; Alastruey 2011); (f) PWDB at population scale. Report openBF/Boileau's relative RMS, max, systolic and diastolic errors so results are comparable.
- PWDB's PDDL licence makes it the one reference dataset found here that can be redistributed in the MIT repository's fixtures without restriction; openBF's reference files are under Apache-2.0 (with attribution/NOTICE obligations); Boileau's original data licence is unknown.

### Gaps

- The parameters of Boileau case 1 and the six schemes' group assignments (by authorship: DG–Alastruey/KCL, LCG–Swansea, GLS–Blanco/LNCC, FV–Müller, MacCormack–Fossan/Hellevik/NTNU, STM–Donders/Huberts) are **[unverified]** inference.
- No public venous benchmark with reference data, equivalent to Boileau's, was found (see Section 5).

## 4. Coupling 0D/1D to 3D

### Takeaway
3D models of an image-derived region are closed by 0D outlets (RCR/impedance/coronary, Vignon-Clementel et al. 2006) or 1D networks (Formaggia et al. 2001; Blanco et al. 2007), coupled implicitly and modularly (Esmaily Moghadam et al. 2013) with backflow stabilisation (Esmaily Moghadam et al. 2011); the theory is the "geometric multiscale" programme of Quarteroni and colleagues (reviewed 2016). The same 0D/1D code that runs alone should expose an interface that returns pressure for a given flow (and its derivative) so that it can serve as a 3D boundary condition.

## 5. Veins: collapsible tube laws, venous valves, gravity and posture, the calf muscle pump, venous return

### Takeaway
Veins differ from arteries in three ways that a solver must carry: a tube law valid through collapse (very compliant when A < A0, area falling towards zero with negative transmural pressure), valves that make flow one-directional, and hydrostatic gravity with external (muscle) pressure. The 1D veins literature is led by Toro, Müller and colleagues (global closed-loop with detailed head and neck veins, 2014; the arterial-venous ADAVN network, 2023; exact Riemann solutions including collapse, 2013/2017/2024), by Mynard & Smolich (whole adult circulation including venous networks and portal veins, 2015; a valve model for 0D and 1D, 2012), and by the lower-limb studies of Keijsers et al. 2015 and Ferlito et al. 2026 (valves, perforators, calf muscle pump, compression). Classical collapsible-tube mechanics comes from Shapiro 1977, Pedley and Brook, and Grotberg & Jensen's review.

### Cited Findings
**Collapsible tubes and tube laws**
- Shapiro 1977, "Steady flow in collapsible tubes" (J Biomech Eng 99(3):126–147), is the classical treatment of flow in tubes that can collapse — [Shapiro 1977](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3426281)

- The venous tube law in current use (ADAVN 2023, after Müller & Toro 2014): p = p_ext + p_tm, with p_tm^ven = K[(A/A0)^m − (A/A0)^n] + K_m (A0/A)·∂A/∂t + p0^ven (the viscoelastic term as extracted from the text; check eq. 6), where K is the effective wall stiffness, m = 10 describes passive stiffening and n = −3/2 collapse; dural sinuses (which do not collapse) use m = 1/2, n = 0; reference pressure p0^ven = 6666.66 dyn cm⁻² (≈ 5 mmHg); K is set from a wave speed interpolated between c_min and c_max by venous radius (eq. 44). ### Inferences
- With the verified venous law p_tm = K(α^m − α^n), α = A/A0, m = 10, n = −3/2 (ADAVN; Müller & Toro 2014), the wave speed c = √((A/ρ) dp/dA) = √((K/ρ)(m α^m − n α^n)) stays positive for all α > 0 (both terms are positive since n < 0), so the system stays strictly hyperbolic through collapse; c is smallest at α* = (n²/m²)^{1/(m−n)} ≈ 0.72 and grows without bound both as α → 0 (the collapsed tube stiffens) and for α > 1. Slow waves near α* are where flow limitation (speed index u/c → 1) is to be expected. This is a short derivation from the cited law, not a quoted result. The (m, n) = (1/2, 0) case is the elastic √A arterial law, so one tube-law class with per-edge (K, m, n) covers arteries, veins and sinuses.
- Gravity enters the 1D momentum equation as a source term ρ g · ê_z A per unit length along the vessel axis (standard; not tied to a source read here), so the image-derived graph must carry each edge's direction in the body frame and a posture rotation; Keijsers 2015 and Ferlito 2026 are the validation targets for posture and muscle pump.
- A venous valve in 1D is a junction whose loss coefficient depends on a valve state variable driven by the pressure difference (Mynard et al. 2012); implement valves as a junction type, not as an edge property, so they also serve heart valves in the 0D heart.
- For venous verification there is no Boileau-style dataset; use exact Riemann solutions with collapse (Spiller 2017; Toro & Siviglia 2013), Brook–Falle–Pedley's steady and roll-wave solutions, and the published waveforms of Müller & Toro 2014 (PC-MRI head/neck veins) and Keijsers 2015 as qualitative and semi-quantitative targets.

## 7. Latest work, 2023–2026: surrogates, patient-specific models from imaging, uncertainty quantification

### Takeaway
Since 2023 the field has moved on three fronts: (i) learned surrogates and corrections of 1D models — graph neural networks trained on 3D data (Pegolotti et al. 2024), physics-informed GNNs (Sen et al. 2024), neural differential equations (Csala et al. 2025), physics-informed neural operators for Bayesian inference (Ryan et al. 2026), ML bifurcation losses (Rubio et al. 2025); (ii) automated, image-driven 0D/1D models and open solvers (Pfaller 2022 → svZeroDSolver 2025; openBF 2024; first_blood 2023; hybrid 1D–0D networks, Ghitti 2023; the ADAVN arterial–venous network 2023); (iii) uncertainty quantification and calibration (Bayesian Windkessel calibration, Richter 2025; GP vs PCE emulators, Paun 2025; patient-specific parameter selection, Taylor-LaPole 2025). Venous 1D modelling of the lower limb appeared in 2026 (Ferlito et al.).

### Cited Findings
- Pegolotti et al. 2024: a GNN predicts pressure and flow at centreline nodes iteratively from an initial condition, trained on 3D simulations; errors below 3% for pressure and flow given enough training data, better than physics-based 1D models while efficient at inference — [Pegolotti et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2023.107676); LSTM + GNN extension: [Iacovelli et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/BHI62660.2024.10913800)
- Sen et al. 2024: physics-informed GNNs on the arterial graph (edges as 1D lines, nodes with velocity and area waveforms), trained with inlet/outlet velocity and diastolic areas plus physics in the loss; R² consistently above 0.99, comparable to discontinuous Galerkin — [Sen et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmpb.2024.108427)
- Csala, Mohan, Livescu & Arzani 2025: physics-constrained coupled neural differential equations that swap space and time (marching in space, exploiting periodicity), more accurate than FEM 1D models across inlet waveforms and stenosis ratios — [Csala et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2024.109644)
- Ryan, Taylor-LaPole, Olufsen, Vyshemirsky & Husmeier 2026: DeepONet, POD-DeepONet and FNO surrogates of a 17-vessel 1D network with structured-tree outflow, physics-informed losses (conservation, PDE residuals, bifurcation conditions), a Wasserstein autoencoder for inflow waveforms, and MCMC inference for two Fontan patients from sparse 4D-flow MRI; physics-informed FNO best — [Ryan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10439-026-04219-1)
- Rubio et al. 2025: ML model of bifurcation pressure differences, more accurate than commonly used loss models; preliminary implementation in a reduced-order solver (CinC 2024) — [Rubio et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2024.109420); [Rubio et al. 2024 CinC](https://doi.org/10.22489/CinC.2024.191)
- Reduced-order modelling of cardiovascular haemodynamics (book chapter, 2024): Pfaller, Pegolotti, Pham, Rubio & Marsden — [Pfaller et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-323-95484-6.00016-6)
- Ghitti, Blanco, Toro & Müller 2023: construction of hybrid 1D–0D networks for efficient and accurate simulation — [Ghitti et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/fld.5149)
- Benemerito et al. 2024 (openBF with viscoelasticity, GUI, multiscale validation); Wéber et al. 2023 (first_blood); Menon et al. 2025 (svZeroDSolver) — see Sections 2 and 6.
- Müller et al. 2023 (ADAVN arterial–venous network); Ferlito et al. 2026 (lower-limb venous 1D) — see Section 5.
- Alastruey et al. 2023 (VascAgeNet review): arterial pulse-wave modelling and analysis for vascular-age studies — [Alastruey et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpheart.00705.2022)
- Uncertainty and calibration: Richter et al. 2025 (SMC Windkessel posteriors on optimised 0D surrogates; open software and data); Paun, Colebank & Husmeier 2025 (GP slightly outperform PCE emulators of two 1D pulmonary models, forward and inverse; code and data at LMihaelaPaun/Emulation_GP_PCE, licence not stated); Taylor-LaPole et al. 2025 (MRI-informed 1D network for single-ventricle/HLHS vs DORV patients with parameter inference of compliance, WSS and wave intensity; a correction was published the same year) — [Richter et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2024.0223); [Paun et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2024.0222); [Emulation_GP_PCE repo](https://github.com/LMihaelaPaun/Emulation_GP_PCE); [Taylor-LaPole et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2024.0663); [correction](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2025.0649)
- Classical UQ foundations still cited: Eck et al. 2016 guide to UQ and sensitivity analysis for cardiovascular applications; Melis, Clayton & Marzo 2017 Bayesian sensitivity of a 1D model with GP emulators; Fossan et al. 2018 UQ for computational FFR — [Eck et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/cnm.2755); [Melis et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/cnm.2882); [Fossan et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13239-018-00388-w)
- Earlier ML landmarks: physics-informed neural networks predicting arterial pressure from 4D-flow MRI with 1D physics (Kissas et al. 2020); ML detection of stenoses and aneurysms on a virtual-patient database (Jones et al. 2021) — [Kissas et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2019.112623); [Jones et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10237-021-01497-7)
- Simulation-based inference for cardiovascular models (Wehenkel et al. 2023, arXiv:2307.13918; preprint, cited in the openBF paper) — [arXiv 2307.13918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13918) **[not verified beyond the openBF reference list]**
- A real-time deep surrogate of a 1D arterial solver for personalised central haemodynamics (Anagnostopoulos et al., arXiv 2604.03197, April 2026) — [arXiv 2604.03197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03197) **[seen only as a search result; authors and content unverified]**

### Inferences
- The pattern to adopt: keep the physics-based 0D/1D solver as the reference (verified by Sections 3 and 5), and treat learned components as replaceable closures at known weak points — junction losses (Rubio 2025), curvature/3D effects (Pegolotti 2024; Csala 2025) — or as emulators for inference (Ryan 2026; Paun 2025; Richter 2025). Each learned closure needs the physics solver to generate its training data, so the solver comes first.
- The image-to-graph-to-model pipeline is now standard in the Marsden group's open tools (Pfaller 2022; svZeroDSolver 2025); its 72-model test set and the Vascular Model Repository are the natural external checks for image-derived networks.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 machine-learning surrogate specific to venous networks was found.
- Iacovelli 2024 is a conference paper (IEEE BHI); I verified only its metadata.
- I did not survey 2023–2026 work on patient-specific 1D models in the coronary and cerebral domains in depth beyond ADAVN and the papers above.
