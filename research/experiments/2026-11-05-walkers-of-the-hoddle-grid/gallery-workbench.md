### Walkers of the Hoddle Grid in the Urban Science Workbench

The Urban Science Workbench's *Human flow* plug-in ([its README](../applications/urban-science-workbench/plugins/human-flow/README.md)) draws the walkers of the urban-science domain's movement model on its 3D map of Melbourne, moving with the workbench's clock. The district is Landscape Dynamics' middle of the Hoddle Grid: Elizabeth Street to Russell Street, Flinders Street to Lonsdale Street, 356 junctions and 399 segments of the city model. Each walker is an agent with an origin and a target. It chooses each next segment by the logit over every walk to its target, walks at its own speed, slows in a crowd, waits at red where two main streets cross and waits at a full street's mouth. The walkers depart in the district-economy flow study's patterns: through trips, arrivals at work, departures, trips between buildings and visits to shops. These are calibrated hour by hour of the week to what the City's 15 counters in the district count each way (R² 0.74 each way, 0.74 both ways together). The engine runs a day in under a minute on six threads, the same on any number, and the page moves each walker between its places every ten seconds. Everything drawn is a model; the counts it stands beside are records. This is a study of methods on a real place, not a planning proposal.

It sees the counter on the Mall each epoch, low, as expected or high, and its belief moves toward the larger crowd. It chose one-way, then open, then closed. Held throughout, the regimes would have cost 2,986 (open), 2,999 (one-way) and 3,000 (closed): person-hours, with those in crush counted ten times. Its sequence cost 2,993. With 12 simulations a decision it planned each ten-minute epoch in at most 90 s on a machine shared with other work (9 to 17 s when it was free), 6.7 times faster than the street's own time.
