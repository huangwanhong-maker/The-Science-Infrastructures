# Fitting Neurons, and the Repertoire of Their Firing

*Status: draft (2026-11-05).* Research and learning: a case study of the search foundation heuresis ([ADR 0073](../../../development/decisions/0073-the-search-foundation-heuresis.md)) on the neuroscience domain's spiking neurons.

## 1. The Question

A model neuron is fitted to recordings by searching its parameters, and each simulation of it costs time and has no gradient. Two things decide whether such a fit means anything: whether a search finds parameters that reproduce the behaviour, and whether the behaviour determines the parameters at all (their identifiability). A third question is the converse of fitting: which behaviours can the model produce, and where in its parameter space does each live?

This study asks all three of the adaptive exponential integrate-and-fire neuron (AdEx; Brette and Gerstner 2005), with a truth known exactly. The target behaviour is generated by the same model at hidden parameters, so a perfect fit exists and its parameters are known; whatever is not recovered is not recoverable from that behaviour, or not found by that search.

1. **Fitting.** Four parameters of adaptation and reset are fitted, by four searches of heuresis, to two descriptions of the behaviour: the firing rates (the initial and steady f–I curves), and the spike times themselves.
2. **Identifiability.** The error is mapped on two planes through the truth, and the recovered parameters are compared with the hidden ones.
3. **Illumination.** MAP-Elites (Mouret and Clune 2015) varies six parameters of the cell under one current and keeps, for every kind of firing on a grid of (rate, irregularity), the cell nearest a reference neuron that fires so. Each cell of the map is named by its firing class.

## 2. The Method

**The model and its simulation.** `neuroscience::snn::models::Adex`, simulated by the domain's network engine (`snn::Network`) on its grid of h = 0.1 ms with the model's own integrator, Dormand and Prince's adaptive method at the engine's default tolerance (10⁻⁶ relative to each component's scale). Every evaluation builds a population of one neuron per current, sets each neuron's constant input by `Network::set_bias`, and records its spikes. C_m = 200 pF, g_L = 12 nS, E_L = −70 mV, V_T = −50 mV, Δ_T = 2 mV, the spike cut at V_peak = 0 mV and no refractory period: Naud et al.'s (2008, Table 1) adapting neuron.

**The fitted parameters and the truth.** θ = (a, τ_w, b, V_r), searched in the unit cube: a = 8 nS·x₀, τ_w = 20 ms·25^{x₁} (logarithmic, 20 to 500 ms), b = 200 pA·x₂, V_r = −70 mV + 25 mV·x₃. The truth is Naud et al.'s adapting neuron: a = 2 nS, τ_w = 300 ms, b = 60 pA, V_r = −58 mV.

**The target.** The truth's spike trains under six constant currents, 300 to 800 pA in steps of 100 pA, for 1 s each. At the truth the cell fires 5, 11, 17, 23, 29 and 35 spikes, with initial rates 16.8 to 181.8 Hz and steady rates 4 to 28 Hz.

**The two errors.**
- *Rates.* For each current, the initial rate f₀ = 1/ISI₁ and the steady rate f_ss (the count in [0.5, 1) s over 0.5 s). The error is the mean over the twelve rates of log²((f + 1 Hz)/(f* + 1 Hz)). It is the f–I curves and their adaptation, as fits of adaptation are usually stated.
- *Spike times.* For each current, van Rossum's (2001) distance with a causal exponential kernel of τ = 20 ms, squared and divided by the target train's squared norm: D² = Σᵢⱼ e^{−|tᵢ−tⱼ|/τ} + Σᵢⱼ e^{−|t*ᵢ−t*ⱼ|/τ} − 2Σᵢⱼ e^{−|tᵢ−t*ⱼ|/τ}, up to the factor τ/2 that the ratio cancels. The error is the mean over the six currents.

Both are 0 exactly at the truth, since target and candidate are simulated on the same grid.

**The searches**, each from `heuresis`, driven by `search::run` with the serial executor, 1000 evaluations, five seeds:
- CMA-ES (`strategies::Cma`), mean at the centre of the cube, σ₀ = 0.3, the default λ = 8, bounded by Hansen's box transformation; its own termination criteria may end a run early;
- differential evolution (`strategies::DifferentialEvolution`), rand/1/bin, NP = 20, F = 0.5, CR = 0.9;
- a particle swarm (`swarms::ParticleSwarm`), 20 particles, the inertia rule (w = 0.7298, c₁ = c₂ = 1.49618) on a ring, the configuration the module's README finds convergent beyond two dimensions;
- random search (`search::RandomSearch`), uniform in the cube.

The 40 runs (four searches, two errors, five seeds) were spread over a calx thread pool, one run per thread. Each run's draws are keyed by its seed, error and algorithm (`search::Draws`).

**Two stages.** Each of CMA-ES's five fits of the rates is then refined on the spike times: CMA-ES started at that fit with σ₀ = 0.05 of the box, 600 evaluations.

**The planes.** The two errors on 41 × 41 points of two planes through the truth: (a, b) with τ_w and V_r at the truth, and (τ_w, b) with a and V_r at the truth, each axis evenly spaced in the unit coordinates.

**The repertoire.** Six parameters in the unit cube: C_m = 100 + 200x₀ pF, g_L = 5 + 15x₁ nS, a = 10x₂ nS, τ_w = 10·50^{x₃} ms, b = 250x₄ pA, V_r = −70 + 30x₅ mV, with E_L, V_T, Δ_T and V_peak as above, under 500 pA for 1 s.
- *Descriptors:* log₁₀ of the mean rate over the second, in [0.3, 2.5] (2 to 316 Hz), and the coefficient of variation of the interspike intervals, in [0, 1.5]; a grid of 44 × 30 cells (`diversity::Grid`).
- *Failures:* a cell with fewer than three spikes, or descriptors outside the box, is a failed evaluation with its reason, which the archive refuses.
- *Quality:* the Euclidean distance in the unit cube to Naud et al.'s tonic neuron (C 200 pF, g_L 10 nS, a 2 nS, τ_w 30 ms, b 0, V_r −58 mV), minimised. Each elite is therefore the smallest change of that reference that fires so.
- *MAP-Elites* (`diversity::MapElites`): 500 initial samples, then candidates bred 64 at a time from the archive as it stands, by Gaussian mutation (σ = 0.05, reflected at the walls) and, with probability ½, the iso+line operator (σ_iso = 0.01, σ_line = 0.2) before it; 20,000 evaluations on a pool of eight threads (`search::Pool`).
- *Random search:* 20,000 uniform samples, offered to an archive on the same grid.

**The firing classes**, read from the intervals d₁ … d_m (m ≥ 2) of a cell's second under 500 pA, in this order (after the patterns of Naud et al. 2008, Fig. 4, and the domain's checks of them):
1. *bursting*: at least four intervals, the longest more than three times the shortest, at least two long intervals (above half the longest) and no fewer short ones;
2. *initial burst*: at least four intervals, the largest ratio of successive intervals above 2.5 and among the first three, and every later interval long;
3. *adapting*: the last interval more than 1.5 times the first;
4. *fast*: none of these, at 100 Hz or more;
5. *tonic*: the rest.

**The exemplars.** For each class, the elite whose descriptors lie nearest the median descriptors of the class's cells (each descriptor scaled by its range), simulated again for 600 ms with its potential recorded every 0.3 ms. Simulated again from the parameters as `elites.csv` rounds them, each exemplar keeps its class and its descriptors to the fourth decimal (`exemplars.csv`, columns `*_again`).

## 5. The Data

No external data. Every spike train is simulated by the neuroscience domain's engine from the parameters above; the target is the model's own behaviour at the hidden parameters. The reference neurons are those of Naud, Marcille, Clopath and Gerstner (2008, Table 1).

## 6. The Results

### 6.1 Fitting the Rates

The rates are fitted exactly: all twelve rates of the fit equal the target's on the grid (error 0) in 8 of 10 runs of CMA-ES and DE.

| Search | Runs at error 0 | First evaluation below 10⁻³ | Best error of the other runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| CMA-ES | 3 of 5, at evaluations 391–490 | 211–339 | 6.6 × 10⁻⁴ (seed 2, stopped at 888), 2.1 × 10⁻⁵ (seed 5) |
| DE | 5 of 5, at evaluations 810–867 | 304–388 | — |
| PSO | 0 of 5 | 388–967 | 6.4 × 10⁻⁵ to 2.9 × 10⁻⁴ |
| random | 0 of 5 | never | 6.0 × 10⁻³ to 2.0 × 10⁻² |

CMA-ES ended four of its runs before the budget by its own criteria (at 680, 728, 888 and 896 evaluations): the error is a step function of the parameters, constant wherever the spike counts and the first interval on the grid do not change, so a run reaching error 0 or a plateau sees no further change.

### 6.2 What the Rates Determine

The eight exact fits of the rates agree with the truth in V_r to 0.26% (−57.97 to −57.85 mV), in τ_w to 2.7% (291.8 to 299.5 ms) and in b to 5.0% (60.3 to 63.0 pA), but in a only to 39% (1.22 to 1.91 nS). Across them, b and a are almost exactly linearly related, b ≈ 67.9 pA − 3.96 a (correlation −0.9986): a smaller subthreshold adaptation a is compensated by a larger spike-triggered adaptation b, and the rates cannot tell them apart. On the (a, b) plane through the truth, 39 of the 1681 points have a rate error below 10⁻² and 5 below 10⁻³, along a valley that runs in that direction (search-41). On the (τ_w, b) plane the valley trades τ_w against b as well (the best b falls from 110 to 40 pA as τ_w rises from 100 to 500 ms), but its floor rises away from the truth: the smallest error on the plane's column is 0.12 at τ_w = 100 ms, 2.2 × 10⁻³ at 263 ms and 2.2 × 10⁻² at 500 ms. Within the valley the rates still fix τ_w; they do not fix a.

### 6.3 Fitting the Spike Times

The spike times determine all four parameters, but their error is a needle: on the (a, b) plane only the grid point at the truth itself has an error below 10⁻² (a step of 0.2 nS in a already costs 0.34), and elsewhere the error lies near 0.4–0.5, the level of trains that agree roughly in their rates but not in their timing.

- **Alone**, the searches mostly fail. All five CMA-ES runs converge to another basin, a = 4.1–7.2 nS and τ_w = 398–500 ms, at errors 0.404–0.428. Two DE runs come near the truth: seed 3 at 0.037 (a 2.009 nS, τ_w 299.3 ms, b 60.06 pA, V_r −58.13 mV) and seed 1 at 0.117. PSO's best is 0.284, random search's 0.466–0.501.
- **In two stages** (the rates first, then the spike times from there), CMA-ES recovers every parameter in 4 of 5 runs, to spike errors of 3.1 × 10⁻³ to 7.6 × 10⁻³: a 1.9976–1.9987 nS, τ_w 299.88–300.43 ms, b 59.94–60.03 pA, V_r −58.03 to −57.96 mV, every relative error at most 0.14%. The fifth (seed 1) started at the fit of the rates closest in spike times (error 0.150), sampled nothing as good with σ₀ = 0.05, and drifted to another basin (0.392).

The rates leave a one-dimensional ambiguity and a broad valley that every search but random descends; the spike times remove the ambiguity but give a target too narrow to find unaided. Each description does what the other cannot.

### 6.4 The Repertoire

MAP-Elites filled 983 of the 1320 cells (74.5%, QD-score 1754.8 with offset 2.5), random search 798 (60.5%, QD-score 1338.6), with the same budget of 20,000 evaluations (78 s on eight threads for MAP-Elites). Of MAP-Elites's evaluations, 350 fired fewer than three spikes and 2915 left the descriptor box, against 485 and 3053 for random search.

| Class | MAP-Elites's cells | Random search's cells | Exemplar |
|---|---|---|---|
| bursting | 494 | 327 | 42 Hz, CV 1.06: b 71 pA, τ_w 94 ms, V_r −46.7 mV |
| adapting | 370 | 358 | 23 Hz, CV 0.36: b 69 pA, τ_w 188 ms, V_r −53.8 mV |
| tonic | 54 | 48 | 11 Hz, CV 0.07: g_L 19.9 nS, a 6.8 nS, τ_w 14 ms, b 9 pA |
| initial burst | 50 | 54 | 8 Hz, CV 0.54: b 115 pA, τ_w 341 ms, V_r −59.7 mV |
| fast | 15 | 11 | 174 Hz, CV 0.01: b 1.5 pA, V_r −51.8 mV |

The classes occupy regions of the map that their definitions do not force. Tonic firing lies at CV below 0.25 at rates from 3 to 99 Hz. Adapting firing spans every rate (3 to 316 Hz) at moderate CV, and at low rates reaches CV 1.45. Initial bursts occur only between 5 and 12 Hz. Bursting takes the high CVs (0.41 to 1.50) and, above 20 Hz, most of the map; 91% of the bursting cells reset above −50 mV, against 7% of the tonic ones, as in Naud et al.'s regular-bursting neuron (V_r −46 mV above V_T −50 mV). MAP-Elites's gain over random search is in the bursting cells (494 against 327); the rarer classes (initial burst, fast) it found no more often than uniform sampling did.

## 8. The Caveats

- **A model fitted to itself.** The target has no noise and comes from the model being fitted, on the same grid; a recorded neuron would add noise, a model error, and a resolution of its own. The identifiability found here (a and b trade off under rates; spike times pin them) is a property of this model and these six currents. Other protocols (steps, ramps, noisy currents) would identify more.
- **Plateaus.** Both errors are piecewise constant in the parameters, since spikes fall on a 0.1 ms grid; error 0 on the rates holds on a set of parameters, not at a point. Precise spike times between grid points (the domain's open question 2) would smooth the spike-time error.
- **One budget, five seeds.** The comparison is of four algorithms at their defaults (PSO in its convergent configuration) and one budget; five runs give the spread, not a distribution. CMA-ES's early ends are its tutorial's criteria, not a failure of the run.
- **The two-stage step size.** σ₀ = 0.05 was chosen once; the one failure shows the stage's start mean is never evaluated. Injecting the start point (`Cma::inject`) would make the refinement unable to end worse than it began.
- **The classifier.** The firing classes are this record's rules, after Naud et al.; cells near a rule's boundary (a rate of 100 Hz, a ratio of 1.5) are classed by it, not by eye. The descriptors are read from one second, so slow adaptation counts as irregularity, and below 10 Hz the rate takes only whole numbers of spikes, which leaves empty columns in the map.
- **Quality.** The distance to a reference neuron, in the unit cube's coordinates, depends on the ranges chosen for the parameters; it says which change is smallest in those units, not in any physiological sense.
- **Wall times** were measured on a machine shared with other work.
